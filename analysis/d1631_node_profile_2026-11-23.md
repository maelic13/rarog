# D.16.3.1: the kernel's node beside Stockfish 16's, function by function — profile, 2026-11-23

The card PLAN D.16.3.1 asks for: a fresh profile of the head on E.0's 281
game positions beside Stockfish 16 classical's, the two matched item by
item in three blocks, the share of quiescence nodes in each tree, and
D.7.8.5's counters on the head at `3+0.03`. Zero games; no engine source
changed. The evidence layer is CPU samples, nanoseconds a node and
counters, which are not NPS reads and not Elo.

## Method

- **Rarog:** the shipped form of the head, a pext PGO build of `4ea670f`
  (engine source as at E.0's close) by `cargo xtask build --tier pext`
  with `CARGO_PROFILE_RELEASE_DEBUG=2`, `id name Rarog
  dev-20261123-4ea670f2`. `bench 13` **3,694,298 / EBF 2.324**, the
  fingerprint. Archived this time with its PDB:
  `rarog-dev-20261123-4ea670f2-windows-x64-pext.exe` (SHA-256
  `1524818e…d0fe8`, byte-identical to the dist build) and `rarog.pdb`
  (`830ec196…2ee93`, GUID `4D25BE5C-…-DAEFBD219406`, the exe's CodeView
  record names it by its bare file name, so the pair symbolises on its
  own).
- **Stockfish 16:** E.0's `sf16-classical-g.exe` (`sf_16` at `68e1e9b`,
  g++ 16.2.0, `-O3 -flto`, debug information, no PGO; `40b003bd…709f6c`)
  and its 24,714 samples (`sf16_s1.txt` to `sf16_s3.txt`); no more were
  needed, since the smallest item this card turns on is several hundred
  samples.
- **Workload, both engines:** Stockfish bench's protocol on the 281
  positions (`sample_positions.jsonl`, the same FENs as
  `sample_fens.txt`): one process, one thread, Hash 128, `ucinewgame` once,
  `go nodes 150000` a position, the table carried. Rarog's side is
  `drive_nodes.py`; four passes under D.7.8.12's sampler
  (`sampler_games.py`, about 800 samples a second, the instruction pointer
  only), 42,055 samples.
- **Rates (to turn shares into nanoseconds):** E.0's `speed_load.py`
  pointed at the head, `go nodes 150000`, one worker, two passes that agree
  within 0.1%: the head **2.423M nodes a second, 412.7 ns a node**;
  Stockfish 16 HCE, the rated PGO binary, **3.214M, 311.2 ns**; the
  profiled debug build 3.030M (330 ns, 6% slower than the rated one). As
  in E.0, Stockfish's shares come from the debug build and are applied to
  the rated binary's rate.
- **Host:** idle before the timed reads (0% to 6% CPU, no engine, harness,
  build or game process); the PC left alone through them.

### The attribution rule, one for both engines

`attribution.py`, applied by `rg_funcs.py` (Rarog, function names from the
PDB) and `sf_funcs.py` (Stockfish, function names from each frame's file
and line through `cpp_funcs.py`, since the LTO build's DWARF leaves most
inlined frames unnamed). A sample's inline chain is walked from the leaf
outward; frames that are generic (the language's library, bit operations,
attack-table lookups, plain board accessors, the kernel's inline rule
arithmetic, history *reads*) are skipped, so their cost goes to the
function that asked for them; the sample is charged to the first frame
that is not generic. An out-of-line generic function, the outermost frame,
is charged by its own name (Stockfish's `Position::attackers_to`). The
node bodies are split by the source line of their frame into the phases of
Stockfish's numbered steps (`search()` steps 1–21; `compact_negamax` by
the matching line ranges at `4ea670f`). `__chkstk` is node frame set-up.
History *updates* are their own item.

Where the boundaries differed they were moved to one side before this
table: the kernel scores quiet moves from its picker and its node,
Stockfish in `MovePicker::score`, and both are P1; Stockfish computes its
threat maps at the top of its quiet scoring and the kernel at every node,
and both are P4; Stockfish's history reads are inline in `search()` and
the kernel's in its rules, and both are charged to the line that reads.

**Normalised by the nodes each part serves.** Quiescence nodes are 32.7%
of the kernel's tree and 46.9% of Stockfish's (below), so the kernel has
67.3% full-width nodes against 53.1%. Per full-width node the node body
(S1–S9) costs 93.7 ns against 89.1 and the history updates 14.6 against
13.9; per quiescence node the quiescence body costs 18.8 against 17.8. The
node body's +15.7 ns a node is the tree's shape, not a heavier node.

**Quiet scoring.** The kernel scores **5.00 quiets a node**, Stockfish
3.27 (plus 0.58 evasion moves, in P2): 1.5 times as many, from the same
shape. A quiet costs **9.73 ns against 3.31**: of the +37.9 ns, 5.8 is the
count and 32.1 the cost a quiet. The hot instructions are the multiplies
that consume the history loads (`rules.rs:2521`–`2525` and the multiply
inlined into it, 6.8% of samples; the four continuation reads touch one
4.5 MB table). A kernel quiet reads six tables (the threat-indexed main
history, the pawn history and four continuations) and five context bits;
a Stockfish 16 quiet reads five and one threat term. Over the whole
ordering block (P1–P3) the kernel pays 14.1 ns a move scored against 9.3.

## What is the tree's form

Today's gap is **101.5 ns a node**, not E.0's 117: the head reads 4.6%
faster than E.0's kernel binary on these positions (2.423M against
2.316M), and Stockfish 311 ns against E.0's 315. It splits:

- **The tree's shape, about 22 ns:** the kernel's tree is two thirds
  full-width nodes against Stockfish's half, so its node body (+15.7),
  history updates (+2.4) and quiet count (+5.8, 5.00 quiets a node against
  3.27) are higher a node while a full-width node costs within 5% of
  Stockfish's (93.7 against 89.1 ns), and a quiescence node within 6%; the
  quiescence body gives 2.2 back.
- **The kernel's own mechanisms, which Stockfish 16 does not have, about
  54 ns:** the correction tables (14.3), the threat maps every node (7.7),
  and the dearer quiet (32.1: the pawn history, the threat-indexed main
  history, five context terms). These are the search Rarog chose and
  fitted (D.7); removing any changes the tree. Of the 32.1, the load
  latency item 1 targets is the one neutral part, a ceiling of about
  14 ns (3.5% of samples).
- **The evaluation's form, 31.5 ns:** spread over its terms, no neutral
  line; E.4's question.
- **Given back:** the table (−7.3); the board is level (+0.2); the picker
  and the rest net +1.2.

So of the 101.5 ns, at most about 14 is removable without changing the
tree, and only through item 1; about 76 is the tree and its mechanisms
(22 + 54), and 31 is the evaluation.

## Verdict for D.16.3, by its frozen rule

The rule: with no behaviour-neutral item of 1% of the samples or more,
D.16.3 closes `NO_CHANGE`. **Item 1 is neutral and its target is 3.5% of
the samples, so D.16.3 does not close: it keeps one item**, built and
priced after D.16.1 and D.16.2 by the read D.16.2 chooses. I recommend it
as a cheap, bounded item, and I recommend against expecting much: the
ceiling assumes every stalled cycle disappears, and the one analogous
prefetch read nothing. Stop rule for its read: an interval that does not
exclude zero reverts it.

**The band, inferred and not measured:** at RAR-M92's 1.67 Elo for 1% of
node rate, removing all 3.5% of the samples would raise the rate by 3.6%,
about +6 Elo; a gain under 1% of rate is under +2. The band for the pass
is **0% to 3.6% of node rate (0 to +6 Elo)**, which replaces E.0's 4% to
12% (+6 to +20): the search side is three quarters of the gap, but most of
it is the tree.

## Where this contradicts E.0's packet

1. *The evaluation is a sixth of the gap* (95 against 77 ns). Fresh, 107.1
   against 75.6: a third. E.0 set D.7.8.12's shares, taken on 12 other
   games, beside rates read on these positions; the workload is the likely
   cause, not shown.
2. *The table, +19 ns.* Now −7.3: E.0's row predates D.7.8.12's prefetch,
   as it said.
3. *Lever 2, search-side work a node, +6 to +20 (4% to 12% of rate).* The
   function-level split puts the neutral ceiling at about 3.6% of rate;
   the rest of the search-side gap is the tree. Corrected to 0 to +6.
4. *The gap is 117 ns.* Today 101.5 on the head.

E.0's other conclusions hold: the gap is the node's own cost, the search
side is the largest part (three quarters, not two thirds), the board is
level.
