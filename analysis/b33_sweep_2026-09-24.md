# B.3.3 curvature sweep, 2026-09-24

PLAN B.3.3; ledger RAR-S79 (the cluster) and RAR-S80 (the categoricals whose
one adoption the arm now carries). Zero games. This file answers one question
for PLAN rule 4: do the proof cluster's main coordinates show curvature at
fixed nodes, which an SPSA needs in order to have something to find? The
classification rule in section 2 was written and committed before any sweep
point was measured.

## 1. The arm under test

The `b3proof` arm with `SingularTtDepthMargin=2` baked as its own coordinate,
`CoreSingularTtDepthMargin` (section 3). Every sweep point runs on one build,
`cargo build --release --features b3proof,tune`, fingerprint-verified before
use and archived with its hash under `tools/results/b33-sweep/bin/`:
`rarog-b33-tune.exe`, sha256 `0b6799dc2a35d934…eb46`, built at `3ca9aab`
(clean), `bench 13` **7,978,292 / EBF 2.465**.

## 2. Classification rule (frozen before any measurement)

**Coordinates and points.** Seven coordinates, each at 0.5×, 0.75×, 1×, 1.5×
and 2× its default, rounded half away from zero, clamped to the declared
range (no point needs the clamp). `CoreSingDoubleBase` has default 0, so its
points are fixed instead at −40, −20, 0, 40, 80.

| Coordinate | Default | Range | 0.5× | 0.75× | 1× | 1.5× | 2× |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `CoreNmpBase` | 150 | 0..=400 | 75 | 113 | 150 | 225 | 300 |
| `CoreNmpREval` | 1044 | 0..=3072 | 522 | 783 | 1044 | 1566 | 2088 |
| `CoreProbcutBase` | 180 | 50..=400 | 90 | 135 | 180 | 270 | 360 |
| `CoreSingularMargin` | 4 | 1..=12 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| `CoreLmrSingularOffset` | 85 | 0..=300 | 43 | 64 | 85 | 128 | 170 |
| `CoreSingDoubleBase` | 0 | −100..=200 | −40 | −20 | 0 | 40 | 80 |
| `CoreSingTripleBase` | 16 | 0..=80 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 24 | 32 |

The first five are PLAN's sweep coordinates; the double and triple bases
were added by amendments 5 and 6 of `analysis/b3_research_2026-09-23.md`.
One coordinate moves at a time; the others stay at their defaults. The 1×
point is one run shared by all seven.

**Measurements per point**, run one at a time on an idle host:
- `bench 13` nodes. The engine gets `setoption` and then `bench 13` under
  the watchdog driver, and the total comes from the driver's log.
- WAC solved at 100,000 nodes: `tools/diag/fixed_budget_probe.py nodes
  100000 src/wac.epd` with the option in the engine spec. The probe stops
  on an option the engine does not report, and a bench log that contains
  `No such option` is a failed run, not a point.

Every number in the tables comes from a script over these outputs.

**Rule.** For each coordinate:
- The *best point* is the one with the most WAC solved, ties broken by fewer
  bench nodes.
- The best point *beats an end* when it solves at least 2 more WAC positions
  than that end. It also beats the end when its bench nodes are at least 3%
  fewer (`N_best ≤ 0.97 × N_end`) and it solves no fewer WAC positions.
- **Curved:** the best point is interior (0.75×, 1× or 1.5×) and beats
  both ends.
- **Monotone:** not curved, and the better end (by the same ordering) beats
  the other end.
- **Flat:** neither.

**What it does not decide.** Fixed-node WAC and bench nodes are not Elo.
RAR-S80 has just shown that a −11.5% bench and +10 WAC switch reads +3.1 ± 9.1
in games. A curved coordinate shows only that the surface has an interior
the SPSA could find. It does not show that the SPSA will gain. The SPSA
surface is registered separately (RAR-S81, not yet written). PLAN rule 4
reads this table as activation and curvature evidence.
