# Coda read, 2026-11-23

A reading of https://github.com/adamtwiss/coda (commit `20d2417`,
2026-11-16) for what Rarog can learn from it, chiefly how it attributes its
sources and discloses what it took, which the maintainer wants as the model
for D.11 (maintainer request 2026-11-23). Coda is not a donor (`PROCESS.md`,
*The independence boundary*): this note records its documents, its rules,
its licence episode and a few measured search results. PLAN owns what this
produced: D.11's sub-steps, E.0's leads and Phase F's licence notes. Nothing
here is legal advice.

## The engine

- Rust, GPL-3.0-or-later, started in January 2026 as a Go engine and
  rewritten in Rust in March; every line written by Claude Code, directed,
  tested and reviewed by one person, which its README says in its opening
  paragraph.
- NNUE: HalfKA with 16 king buckets plus threat input features, a 1024-wide
  accumulator with pairwise activation, two small int8 hidden layers and
  material-bucketed outputs, trained with a fork of bullet on LC0 data and
  its own. Lazy accumulator, Finny tables, incremental threat deltas,
  runtime-dispatched AVX2, AVX-512 VNNI and NEON.
- A full modern search with about 130 tunable parameters, Lazy SMP over a
  five-slot lockless table, Syzygy through `shakmaty-syzygy` with a probe
  cache, Polyglot books, binpack datagen.
- Strength by its own reads: lichess about 3000 to 3080; fourth of twenty in
  its local round robin at `10+0.1` behind Stockfish, Reckless and Obsidian.
  Its pool is not anchored to CCRL.
- Testing on its own OpenBench instance, over 5,000 tests; merge commits
  carry the test, the result and the maintainer's approval
  ("#5128 H1, +1.4 ±1.5 @52.4k at [-1.5,0.5]; Adam OK"), and every commit
  that changes search or evaluation carries `Bench: <nodes>`.

## How Coda discloses its sources

The documents, each with one job:

1. **`docs/license_analysis_2026-07-13.md`**, the authoritative record, dated
   in its name and kept as a living document ("later revisions are us
   incorporating new facts, not shifting our position"). Its parts:
   - *Why this exists*: the public report that prompted it, named.
   - *The governing principle*: ideas, algorithms and functional constants
     are not protected expression; source text, names, structure and
     comments are. The test applied throughout: was expression copied, or
     only an idea or a functional constant, and under which licence was the
     source **at the version referenced**.
   - *The licence landscape*: a table of every engine it cites, grouped by
     the licence of the version it read, with dates where a licence changed
     after the reading (Viridithas was MIT through v20 and AGPL from v21).
   - One section per donor: what was taken, what was not, what made it look
     worse than it was (its own comments overstated a port as "verbatim"),
     its view, and what it changed, with commit hashes.
   - The one place it found copied expression (a threat-feature index
     construction modelled on Reckless's), stated plainly and reimplemented.
   - Third-party libraries, separately from the engines whose ideas it used.
   - The repository's structure (study notes quoting other engines' code
     moved to a private repository), and the git history (deliberately not
     rewritten, and said outright not to be clean).
   - Audit scope and status, with what is still to do, and the defensive
     rules adopted.
   - Its position, including "the originality critique has a fair point",
     and an invitation to open an issue with specifics.
2. **`docs/license_compliance_review_2026-07-11.md`**, the first pass, kept
   unedited under a status banner that says what it missed and that the
   later document supersedes it.
3. **`NOTICES.md`**: material from studied engines, as opposed to linked
   code. It reproduces the full MIT notice where constants were taken, and
   says "ideas only" for the rest.
4. **`THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md`**: every linked dependency's notice,
   regenerated from the dependency tree at release time and attached to each
   release.
5. **README**: *Credits* names the tools it relied on (OpenBench, bullet,
   LC0), the training data with its licence (LC0's ODbL notice quoted, and
   why share-alike does not reach the code or the nets), and *Engines we
   learned from* grouped by licence; *License* tells the history in a
   paragraph (briefly labelled MIT, then the AGPL report, the audit, and
   who is thanked for raising it).

What makes it work: it states what was taken without overstating or
understating it, gives each fact one home, separates ideas from linked code
from data, records the licence at the version read, and invites correction.

## Coda's licence episode and the rules it adopted

In July 2026 Andrew Grant reported that parts of Coda resembled Reckless and
Viridithas. Coda's audit found: Reckless-modelled SIMD kernels (removed, then
rewritten from Coda's own scalar code), Reckless's king-bucket and
output-bucket tables (removed; the next net trained on its own layout), the
threat-feature index construction (reimplemented, the feature mapping kept
since the trained net depends on it), and Viridithas's time-management
constants (attributed, exposed to SPSA and retuned on Coda's own search;
about 40% came back bit-identical and the rest within a few percent, which
it reads as evidence that they are functional optima, not expression). It
cost about 3% of NPS on the affected hosts.

The rules, in its `CLAUDE.md` and the analysis:

1. AGPL engines are excluded entirely (Reckless, Viridithas v21 and later,
   Icarus, Raphael, Tcheran); their source was deleted from its machines and
   only their binaries are used for testing.
2. Ideas, never expression, from any engine whatever its licence.
3. No foreign constant crosses, not even as a seed that SPSA will retune:
   every constant is derived from the engine's own quantities, with the
   derivation written beside it.
4. No foreign code, formulas or `file:line` citations in public comments or
   commit messages; cross-engine reading and quoting stays in the private
   research repository.
5. Close the reference before writing.
6. If it happens anyway, delete and rebuild the branch, never amend.
7. Attribute techniques as shared conventions, accurately, neither
   overstating nor concealing.

## What AGPL is, and what it would change for Rarog

Not legal advice; the maintainer decides (D.11.2).

- **AGPL-3.0 is GPL-3.0 plus one section, 13.** A *modified* version that
  users interact with remotely over a network must offer those users its
  complete source. GPL's duties start only when a binary is distributed;
  AGPL's also start when a modified program is run as a service.
- **The two can be combined.** Section 13 of each licence allows a GPL-3.0
  work and an AGPL-3.0 work to be combined and conveyed. Each part keeps its
  own licence, but AGPL's section 13 applies to the combination as a whole.
  So a GPL-3.0 Rarog may contain AGPL-derived parts, provided those parts
  are marked as AGPL with Reckless's copyright notice kept and the whole is
  conveyed so that section 13 is honoured. A repository whose only licence
  statement is GPL-3.0-or-later and which names Reckless only as
  "inspiration" does neither. That labelling gap is the defect today,
  whichever licence Rarog ends up with.
- **Whether Rarog's kernel is a derivative work in the legal sense is
  uncertain.** Its text is Rarog's own (0.34% shared token runs at 2.6.0),
  and ideas and functional constants are not protected. The argument a
  rights holder could make rests on the kernel's whole shape rebuilt from an
  inventory of Reckless's, the time manager taken whole with its constants,
  the constants still at Reckless's values, and the two transcribed places.
  The tracked patches in `tools/oracle-hybrids/` hold Reckless's own source
  as context lines, so they are AGPL material whatever else is decided.
- **What relicensing to AGPL-3.0-or-later would change:**
  - Files: `LICENSE` (AGPL text), `Cargo.toml`'s `license`, README's
    *License*, the shipped archives (D.15.1).
  - Rarog's own duties: as under GPL (ship the licence, offer the source),
    plus section 13: a modified Rarog that others play or query over a
    network (a lichess bot, an analysis service) must offer its source. The
    repository is public, so a link in the bot's profile or the service
    meets it. Rating lists that run the binary on their own machines, and
    tournament hosts, are unaffected, as Reckless's own AGPL entries show.
  - Others' duties: anyone who modifies Rarog and runs it as a service must
    publish their changes. Closed-source GUIs and apps are already excluded
    by GPL; AGPL adds service operators.
  - The maintainer's other engines (Basilisk, Manta, Whitespine are
    GPL-3.0): the maintainer may reuse Rarog's own original code under any
    licence, as its only author (every commit in this repository is
    theirs). Material that came from Reckless carries the question with it
    wherever it goes.
  - Earlier releases stay as they were published (GPL-3.0); 2.5.0 to 2.7.0
    already carried B.2's Reckless constants, which the disclosure names.
  - Compatibility: Fathom (MIT) and the Stockfish-derived design and
    fixtures (GPL-3.0) combine with AGPL as they do with GPL; Rarog links no
    crates.
  - It settles compliance, not originality: the disclosure is owed either
    way (ICGA rule 2, community norms, and the scrutiny agentically developed
    engines now get, of which Coda is the example).
- **What staying GPL-3.0 would take (Coda's route):** remove or reimplement
  every Reckless-derived part from ideas, with constants derived from
  Rarog's own quantities (the clock and its 23 constants, the two thread
  spreads, the eight unreached seeds); relabel or move the patches; change
  PROCESS's boundary so that an AGPL engine teaches ideas only and no
  constant crosses; and turn Phase F's decision of 2026-11-23 (Reckless a
  primary source for the network runtime and trainer pipeline) into ideas
  only. The kernel's shape, Reckless's by D.7's design, would remain the
  point where an argument could still be made, and Reckless could no longer
  be read at its latest commit as PROCESS now prescribes.
- **Asking Reckless's authors** (the maintainer sends) is a courtesy either
  way, and a written permission would settle the question for what it
  covers.

## Rarog's exposure, checked 2026-11-23

- **Reckless's licence by date** (its `LICENSE` history on GitHub): MIT from
  `b00fa6ca2` (2023-04-16); no licence from `a0a239741` (2025-02-22), which
  reserves all rights; AGPL-3.0 from `29d9f6c0c` (2026-01-26). Every
  Reckless commit Rarog cites for its search and clock is from after
  2026-01-26: the oldest, `12af143` (2026-05-31), is the snapshot the 2.6.0
  scans read, and `0756957` (2026-11-07) is AGPL in the local clone. So the
  material Rarog took is AGPL. A Reckless commit cited only for a reading
  (`c3c89eae3`, 2023-02-07) predates any licence and carries nothing into
  the code.
- **The tree:** the repository is public; `LICENSE` and `Cargo.toml` say
  GPL-3.0-or-later; README credits Stockfish and Reckless for
  "inspiration"; Fathom (MIT) is vendored with its licence and not named in
  README; no other third-party code is linked.
- **Tracked AGPL material:** `tools/oracle-hybrids/reckless-0756957-*.patch`
  (three patches, Reckless source in their context lines) and the
  Stockfish patch beside them (GPL-3.0, compatible). Neither is labelled
  with its own licence.
- **Donor citations in public prose:** fifteen tracked files (twelve
  analyses, PLAN and two experiment records) cite donor source files by
  path, most with line numbers. A citation is
  not a copy; whether any of them quotes donor code beyond a few words is
  what D.11.1 reads.
- **The rules:** PROCESS lets constants cross as seeds and allows
  transcription where an algorithm has one natural form; D.7 rebuilt the
  kernel's shape from Reckless's and took its clock whole (D.11, *Named at
  D.7.14*); Phase F now names Reckless a primary source for the network
  runtime and trainer pipeline. Coda's one finding of copied expression was
  in exactly that area: threat-feature indexing and SIMD kernels modelled
  on Reckless's.
- **Sequencing:** the licence question was raised on 2026-10-22, and D.7
  deepened the dependence (2026-11-13 to 11-21) before D.11 settled it. No
  release carries the compact kernel yet; the source is on public `master`
  since PR #20. The fix lands before 2.8.0.

## Search and tooling leads, with Coda's numbers

Coda's results are its own: NNUE, its search, its STC unless stated. They
order Rarog's tests and accept nothing. Already in Rarog and not leads: the
reverse-futility return interpolated toward beta, the multi-cut's
interpolated return, threat-aware quiet history, pawn history, pawn,
non-pawn and continuation correction, hindsight reduction, upcoming
repetition.

1. **Transition correction:** a correction table keyed by
   `hash(ply-1) ^ hash(ply)`, the last move in its position (from, to,
   capture, side), credited to Cinder; weight tunable, in its core set. No
   result in the 50 commits read. Rarog has no such source; a hand-crafted
   evaluation's errors may be larger and more move-shaped than a network's.
2. **LMR by the child's static evaluation against alpha:** the reduction
   adjusted by how far the evaluation after the move sits from alpha
   (#4979, +1.2 ± 1.0 over 124k games). Rarog has no such term.
3. **Gains by removal:** two ablation merges removed terms and gained
   (#4797, #4795, each +1.9 ± 1.8): a searched-count history scale, and
   LMR's expectation-gap and winning-beta terms. Each search feature has an
   on/off switch, and the switch carries its measured cost in a comment
   ("load-bearing: −18 Elo without").
4. **Per-thread root order:** each Lazy SMP thread orders its root by its
   own latest best move (#4957, +1.2 ± 1.4 over 61k games at four threads).
5. **A core flag on each tunable:** routine retunes run only the curated
   core set, so each parameter gets a better signal-to-noise ratio; the rest
   stay loadable for full sweeps. The spec is emitted by the engine
   (`coda tune-spec`) and never committed, since a checked-in copy goes
   stale at the first applied tune.
6. **Speed work gated in games** at `[-1.5, 0.5]` (#5128 +1.4 ± 1.5 over
   52.4k; #5108 +0.3 ± 0.9 over 147.6k).
7. **ARM memory ordering:** shared atomics use acquire and release with
   reader-publish patterns, since x86 hides ordering bugs that show on ARM;
   a table written by `setoption` while helpers read it is atomic, not
   `static mut`. Rarog ships macOS and Windows on ARM and uses 70 relaxed
   and 26 stronger orderings in `src/`.
8. **Network files named by content hash** (`net-<hash>.nnue`), with no
   architecture prefix, since the header already carries it.
