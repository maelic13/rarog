# D.6.30 screen, 2026-11-13: does a later iteration drop a proven win?

PLAN D.6.30 (`analysis/basilisk_check_2026-11-13.md` §1). Each completed
root iteration replaces the root's score and move
(`src/search/mod.rs`, `search_root`); the mate watch stops the search and
keeps nothing, so an iteration that loses a proven win to pruning or a table
artefact replaces it. Basilisk keeps a proven mate against such an iteration.

## The instrument

Diag-only counters in `search_root`, the single-PV driver games use (the
MultiPV driver is not instrumented; nothing changes in a production build):
`iter_mate_win`, completed iterations whose score proves a win;
`iter_mate_win_lost`, later completed iterations in the same search that no
longer do; `iter_mate_win_lost_move`, those of them with a different move;
`search_mate_win_dropped`, searches that proved a win and end without one;
`search_mate_win_dropped_move`, those of them that play a different move from
the last proving iteration's.

## Registered before any replay (frozen 2026-11-13)

Nothing has been run with these counters.

- **Sample:** the same as D.6.29's: the release head's side of the first 200
  games of RAR-M87's head-to-head leg, one process per game, `go nodes` at
  the recorded counts, moves as played
  (`analysis/artifacts/d630-screen-2026-11-13/replay_d630.py`). The mate
  canaries the card named are left out: these games hold hundreds of mates,
  and the canaries are fixed test positions, not game states.
- **The quantity:** `search_mate_win_dropped_move`, searches whose played
  move is not the proven win's; the other four are reported.
- **Stop rule:** zero → `NO_CHANGE` (no played move changes; a score-only
  drop is reported and closes with it). One or more → a repair card, keeping
  the proven move, gated by what it moves.
- **Prediction:** zero searches drop a proven win with a different move;
  P(`NO_CHANGE`) 0.7. Iterations that lose a win at all: a handful, under 1%
  of the proving searches. Basis: a mate proved at one depth is found again
  at the next by the TT move and the mate-distance bounds in almost every
  case; the mate watch ends most searches soon after.
- **Caveat:** `go nodes` is not the game's clock; the mate watch and the
  node budget end searches where the game's time did.

## Results

The diag build's `bench 13` reads 9,077,016, the release's fingerprint:
18 proving iterations, one later iteration that loses the win with the
same move, no search that ends without it.

**The replay** (`replay.json` SHA-256 `f997610e…`, driver `e78f29d0…`):
200 games, 12,520 searches. 267 searches prove a win in some completed
iteration (994 such iterations). In 35 of them a later iteration loses it
(55 iterations, 5 with a different move); 15 searches end without the
proven win, and **3 play a different move** from the proving iteration's
(games 32, 39 and 161). The head won all twelve games in which a search
ended without the win: a dropped win delayed the mate there, it lost no
result.

**Decision by the frozen rule: 3 is one or more, so D.6.30 becomes a
repair card** (PLAN D.6.30, `READY_FOR_IMPLEMENTATION`).

**Calibration.** Predicted zero searches playing a different move and
P(`NO_CHANGE`) 0.7; three did, so the sign of the decision missed. The
iteration-level count, predicted under 1% of proving searches, is 13%
(35 of 267): the assumption that failed is that the TT move and the
mate-distance bounds find a proven win again at the next depth almost
every time. Mates are lost often and recovered mostly, by the end of the
search in 252 of 267.

**What this does not show.** Any change in a result: all twelve games
were won. The cost is time and mate length, which bears on D.6.20's
won-ending thinks. Evidence layer: counts on replayed searches.
