## 3. The four resolutions

**(1) Reductions: the §13.2 invariant over §3.7's scope — upheld.** §3.7
adopts the donor's LMR "for every move class" and its full-depth branch that
reduces a non-PV node's first move by one or two plies; §13.2 freezes
"nothing reduced or omitted at the root, in check, or before one move is
searched". The invariant is the later and more specific text and is what the
cluster's tests construct. The implementation reduces only after the first
searched move (`searched > 0`), never at the root, never in check, from depth
2, with `reduced_depth` floored at one ply and capped at `new_depth + 2`
(`reduced_depth_is_floored_at_one_ply_and_capped_above`). The donor's
full-depth branch is therefore not built and the first move of a non-PV node
is searched at full depth. Consequence, recorded for B.3: the candidate's
tree is larger than the donor's shape at the same coordinates; if B.2.2's
branching window or B.2.3's fit points at over-searched first moves, the
full-depth branch is a registered switch to try there, not a silent addition.

**(2) Direct checks and promotions after the quiet skip — upheld, and the
right reading.** The donor's `!is_direct_check` exempts a direct check from
*triggering* the skip; its picker then emits no quiet at all, so a direct
check ordered after the skip is lost. §13.2 says "direct quiet checks survive
the count skip", which is the stronger, Manta-derived statement (a count skip
dropping an unmade mating quiet was one of Manta's two seed defects). The
picker marks direct checks and non-capturing promotions `survives_skip` and
keeps emitting them; `skipping_quiets_still_emits_direct_checks_and_promotions`
and `a_late_direct_check_survives_the_quiet_skip` (a rook mate ordered last
past the skip at a node far below its window) construct it. Promotions are
noisy in the donor and never skipped there, so including them is
donor-faithful, not an extension.

**(3) Rule-50 damping in the search — upheld; §3.3 required exactly this.**
The table stores the raw eval under a key without the clock, so the raw eval
must not depend on the clock. Under `b2core` the evaluator's damping line is
compiled out and `corrected_eval_parts` damps by `min(clock, 100) / 199` at
the current clock before adding the correction, the donor's order. Tests:
`evaluator_raw_eval_does_not_depend_on_the_halfmove_clock`,
`a_stored_raw_eval_serves_any_halfmove_clock` (stored at clock 0, read at
clock 80, equal to a fresh evaluation and damped),
`rule50_damping_is_the_evaluators_and_material_is_neutral_at_zero`. Every
evaluation consumer in the crate goes through `Searcher::raw_eval`, so no
caller outside the search loses the damping (`rg "\.evaluate\("`). Two loose
ends, neither a defect: the whole-eval cache still keys on the halfmove clock
under `b2core`, which is only a wasted miss when a position recurs at another
clock (B.7); and the texel-only `linear_delta_scale` still mirrors the old
damping, which matters only to a `texel` build, which is never measured.

**(4) KBNK anchor as a five-budget majority — upheld.** The single 60,000-node
playout was chaotic on the accepted engine too (`7N/8/7B/8/8/8/8/2K2k2 w`
mates at three of five budgets there), so the old test measured budget luck,
not conversion. The change is in its own commit (`d7560d8`) with the
justifying measurement in the test's comment, as the rule for relaxing a
correctness test requires; the discrimination survives (the drive scaled to
1/24 mates at 2, 2 and 0 of five). One caution: that accepted-arm position
passes at exactly the majority threshold, so a future accepted-arm change
that flips one budget fails it without a conversion regression. Whoever hits
that should widen the budget set, not lower the majority.

## 4. Findings

No defect. Recorded observations, each with its owner:

- **Fail-low nodes carry a TT move (B.3).** Rarog stores the best-scored move
  at a fail-low; the donor stores none there (`best_move` is set only when
  alpha rises). Three consumers see the difference: IIR (`tt_move.is_null()`),
  razoring's "TT move not quiet" guard and the LMR no-TT-move term fire less
  often here than in the donor. IIR is KEEP-in-B.2, DECIDE-in-B.3; B.3's
  IIR-versus-hindsight measurement should know the population differs for
  this reason. Correction admission is unaffected: the tail rule
  `!(Exact && noisy best)` is equivalent to the donor's `!best_move.is_noisy()`
  because the donor's fail-low `best_move` is null.
- **Dead PVS guard (B.8).** `core/node.rs`, the PV re-search: `if mv ==
  tt_move && ev.depth > 1 { new_depth = new_depth.max(1) }` is the donor's
  guard for a TT move whose negative singular extension (−3 there) drove
  `new_depth` below one. Here the TT move is always the first move, which
  takes the other branch, and the negative extension is −1 at depth ≥ 4, so
  the guard cannot fire. Remove in the cleanup pass; not worth an engine
  commit now.
- **Per-node work the accepted arm does not do (B.7).** `Board::threats()` at
  every interior node after the TT cutoff and at every quiescence node after
  move generation, even when the capture list is empty; `check_info()` built
  twice per node (once for the picker's check-square ordering term, once
  lazily for the pruning and make-move hint); after a quiet skip the picker
  still runs its selection scan over every remaining quiet to find the
  survivors (quadratic in the tail). Each is cheap alone; together with the
  table footprint below they are the first places to look if B.2.2's NPS
  floor fails.
- **Per-thread table footprint (D.2).** Correction: four keyed tables of
  16 buckets × 2 × 65,536 × 2 B = 4 MiB each, plus two continuation tables of
  4.5 MiB; histories: continuation 4.5 MiB, pawn 0.75 MiB, quiet and noisy
  under 0.1 MiB. About 31 MiB per thread, allocated per `ThreadData`. Fine at
  the thread counts B measures; D.2's shared-correction decision should count
  it, and G.1's high-thread work will notice it.

Confirmed equal to the donor, where the handoff's prose could be read two
ways: `last_critical_ply` (the first move inherits, a reduced search and a
later full-depth search pass `ply + 1`, re-searches inherit); the fail-low
parent reward's condition `!ROOT && Upper && (cut_node || PV)` and its
eval-unit thresholds converted (97 → 44, 136 → 62); the searched-move lists
excluding a move that raised alpha; `cutoff_count` reset two plies ahead and
read one ply ahead; the null move zeroing `laterality` and inheriting a null
`mv` from `clear_move`; razoring without an excluded-move guard (the donor
has none either; at singular depths the quadratic margin makes it unreachable
in practice). Seeds spot-checked against §8.3 and the donor: razoring, RFP,
quiet and bad-noisy futility, SEE both branches, hindsight margins, LMP,
history pruning, the good-noisy SEE threshold (`116 → 87`, `47 → 63`), the
victim weight (`14232 / 0.75`), every LMR term, every history bonus and
malus, the correction update clamp, table maxima, the five-bit age and the
replacement refusal. Ablation bits 0–7 are declared on the mechanisms §10
names.

## 5. Speed: a warning for B.2.2, outside the acceptance

Alternating single-build benches of the two hashed executables on the idle
host, five each, no PGO (`nps_alt.txt`): off arm median 2.93 Mnps, candidate 2.09 Mnps, ratio of medians 0.712, per-pair ratios 0.70–0.72. This is not the
registered instrument (the pooled-PGO run of B.2.2) and the candidate's node
mix differs (0.67 quiescence nodes per interior node against 1.02), so the
per-node cost is not directly comparable; but the gap is far outside the
noise of this screen. P5 predicted 0.93–0.97x and named 0.90x as the
falsifier that "sends the `threats()` producer to B.7 before the gate".
Recommendation for B.2.2's order: run the pooled NPS first, since it is the
cheapest falsifier in the screen set; if it reads below 0.90x, profile the
candidate before the 2,000-game paired run so the B.7 ticket is specific
(section 4 lists the suspects), and let the maintainer decide whether B.7's
speed pass precedes B.2.4's gate. Nothing here changes what the arm does.
