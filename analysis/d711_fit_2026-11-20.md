# D.7.11 — the finishing fit: the clock, the surface, the shape and the reads (2026-11-20)

PLAN D.7.11's research, at zero games. It answers five questions and
rewrites the step. Evidence layer: node counts on `bench 13`, clock use and
harness records read from game files, the first fit's own journal, and the
pinned runner's source. None of it is Elo.

## Result

1. **The clock's fifteen soft-limit coordinates stay out of the fit**, at
   the donor's values. D.7.9.1's card asked which clock coordinates the fit
   takes "so that a `3+0.03` fit cannot move the long-control allocation
   unread" and answered all fifteen. That answer is withdrawn.
2. **The surface is 225 coordinates**: 218 of the kernel's 242 seed options
   and seven new ones. Out: sixteen seeds a fit moves in whole units, eight
   inactive on the bench at two depths, and the thread spreads and four
   units by construction.
3. **Rule 7c's shape stands**: 30 games an iteration on 15 slots, two
   steps, `r_end` 0.00155, ceiling 6,000, checkpoints at 2,000 and every
   1,000. About 13.5 hours to the first checkpoint, 6.8 for each further
   1,000 iterations, 40.5 to the ceiling.
4. **`--max-time-losses 0` is dropped.** On the pinned runner a tune that
   passes a fixed fault limit ends on an invalid iteration and cannot be
   resumed. The first fit's journal holds 19 harness stalls over 54 ms, and
   54 ms is the new clock's lowest forfeit slack. One forfeit would end a
   40-hour run for good.
5. **A tune already pauses and resumes cleanly**: one Ctrl+C, then the same
   command. No tooling change. The tune never pauses for a read.
6. **D.7.13 cannot see this fit.** The kernel already reads +63.8 Elo
   against the classic, so the gate passes on the pre-fit state. The fit's
   own contribution is measured once, by a read of theta against the start
   at the stop, with D.7.8's keep rule.
7. **D.7.12 must not fit on the tune's games**: each arm plays a perturbed
   wrapper, so its scores sit on another scale.

## 1. The clock in the fit

**Decision: hold all fifteen (`TmNodesBase` to `TmChangesScale`) at the
donor's values.** The budget's constants stay constants.

### Evidence

- **Three of the fifteen are inert at `3+0.03`.** At `3000+30` no iteration
  of 4,200 sits at `TmNodesFloor`, `TmPvFloor` or `TmEvalFloor`. A fit would
  random-walk them. At `10000+100` `TmPvFloor` binds on 2.1% of 5,125
  iterations: these floors belong to longer searches, which the fit's
  control does not play.
- **The factors' population moves with the control.** The best move has
  held ten iterations or more on 4.5% of iterations at `3000+30` and 9.9%
  at `10000+100`. At the last completed iteration, the one that decides the
  stop, the mean node-share factor is 1.147 against 0.994 and the mean
  stability factor 1.011 against 0.947; the product of the five means is
  1.66 against 1.33. Constants fitted at `3+0.03` are fitted to the first
  population.
- **The score-trend factor is a switch on Rarog.** From each move's final
  score in RAR-S134's games it sits at a clamp on 79% of the kernel's moves
  at `3+0.03` (low 52.9%, high 25.7%) and 75% at `10+0.1`. The band between
  the clamps is 5.7 units wide and the median move-to-move change is 11. Its
  base and slope act on a fifth of moves.
- **Six of the fifteen are one scale.** The soft limit is the bound times
  five factors, so `TmNodesBase`, `TmPvBase`, `TmEvalBase`, `TmChangesBase`
  and the trend's two clamps each scale the same product. A fit cannot tell
  them apart, and their shared gradient is the mean time a move.
- **The one clock fit this engine has played found one gradient, on the
  scale.** RAR-R15's block: thirteen clock coordinates alone, 60,000 games
  at `3+0.03`. Read by rule 7c's test it is live over its first 1,000
  iterations through `TmOptScale` only (z 5.34) and quiet at 2,000
  (`analysis/spsa_method_adoption_2026-11-13.md`). Its gate ended
  unresolved at +0.6 ± 5.8 Elo, with −6.3 ± 11.7 at `10+0.1` and
  −10.4 ± 11.6 at four threads beside it (RAR-R16; that arm also carried a
  stop later read at about −15, so the reads are clouded).
- **The scale's optimum is the control's.** At `3+0.03` 31% of the kernel's
  sides fall under 100 ms and 4.7% of its moves are sent under 100 ms of
  clock; at `10+0.1` none (RAR-S134). The port gained +30.0 at `3+0.03` and
  +18.6 at `10+0.1` (RAR-R34). A `3+0.03` gradient on the time a move is
  partly a gradient on how long to live on the increment, a regime no rated
  control has.
- **The donor's values are tuned and already read.** Two SPSA sessions
  moved every one of them with the donor's search, verified at `8+0.08` and
  `40+0.4`; on Rarog they are the clock RAR-R34 and RAR-S134 measured.

### The four options

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Fit at `3+0.03` with the rest | **No.** Three inert, six one scale, the scale's gradient the control's; the expected gain is a few Elo at the test control and unread elsewhere. |
| Hold at the donor's values | **Yes.** Costs nothing; the fit's arms all play the clock that was measured. |
| A separate long-control run | **Not now.** At `10+0.1` a tune plays about 1,300 games an hour, so 2,000 iterations take about 45 hours, for coordinates whose only measured gradient is the scale. It opens by a trigger, below. |
| Jointly under a guard | **No.** A forfeit guard does not answer the overfit, and the guard itself is the problem of section 4. |

### What would change it

- *Competing explanation:* the donor's constants are misfit to Rarog's
  search (depth, node share, score scale), and a joint fit would gain more
  than it distorts. Not excluded; RAR-R15 is the only direct evidence and it
  is clouded.
- *Cheapest check:* D.7.13's two companions. A `10+0.1` reading below the
  `3+0.03` reading by more than their combined intervals says the clock's
  allocation costs at the longer control.
- *Retry trigger:* that, or a rated control's games showing a factor at one
  end on more than 90% of iterations. The fit is then its own registration
  at `10+0.1` or longer over the coordinates active there, never inside a
  search fit. Until then H.3 holds clock refinements.

Interaction with the search coordinates is one-way and weak: the factors
read the search (node share, best-move changes, score stability), and the
finishing fit moves the search a little. The soft limit does not enter a
node. Nothing is lost by fitting the search first.

## 3. The shape

Rule 7c is kept whole. Each part, from this engine's own run:

- **30 games an iteration.** The smallest even count that fills 15 slots.
  RAR-S128's slots were busy 79.3% of an iteration (23.16 s an iteration,
  a game 9.15 ± 2.15 s). Dealt as 60 games the same durations fill 86.9%,
  10% more games an hour, and to first order SPSA gains the same per game.
  **Not adopted now:** it changes the wrapper's guard, the reader's
  constants and rule 7c's wording ahead of the longest run, for about
  3.5 hours of 40. It is recorded as a candidate amendment to rule 7c,
  to be proven on a smoke before F.6's refit.
- **Two steps.** The first fit's floor was 4.7 Elo a coordinate across a
  perturbation over a 1,000 window (SE 1.28). One step would double it.
- **`r_end` 0.00155, ceiling 6,000.** The schedule the first fit ran on. Its
  noise travel (median 2% of a range), its floor and its gain rate are the
  only measured references on this surface, and another ceiling changes
  every iteration's gain. The ceiling is a cap, not a plan.
- **First checkpoint 2,000, then every 1,000, window 1,000.** The first
  fit's four windows of 500 read +3.9, +3.5, +0.4 and +3.4 SD: one in four
  would have stopped a live tune. Its 1,000 window at 2,000 read +1.4 SD,
  live by one coordinate. Nothing shorter is safe. A residue of that size
  reads quiet more often than not; that is the stated floor (about 10 Elo
  over the next 2,000 iterations), rule 7c's price.
- **The stop.** Quiet, a rail, a fault or the ceiling, and the maintainer's
  `STOP` at a checkpoint. Unchanged.
- **Hours.** 23.16 s an iteration measured (155.4 an hour). The new clock
  spends 0.896 of the budget against 0.851, so about 24.3 s: **13.5 hours
  to 2,000, 6.8 for each further 1,000, 40.5 to 6,000.** The pilot measures
  the rate and the registration restates the hours from it.

*What the surface can be expected to show:* at its stop the first fit
forecast at most +7.3 ± 5.1 Elo for its next 2,000 iterations. Since then
the tree moved (D.7.8) and six coordinates joined. A restart on a fitted
surface read +13.1 ± 5.4 once (RAR-S78) and +1 to +2 once (RAR-S104).

## 4. Stopping and reads

### The fault limit

- A tune that passes a fixed limit ends on an invalid iteration, exit 5,
  and `run_spsa` refuses a checkpoint that holds one
  (`crates/colosseum-cli/src/spsa_driver.rs:262`, `TerminalCheckpoint`, at
  `v0.5.0`). Rule 7c lets the maintainer accept a fault once analysed; an
  invalid run could not be played past it.
- The new clock's floor: over 4,000 kernel sides at `3+0.03` (RAR-R34,
  RAR-S134) the lowest clock sent is 64 ms, the lowest slack (clock plus
  margin less the charged time) 54 ms, and the largest hold 10.9 ms. The
  hybrid's lowest slack over 120,060 sides was 99 ms.
- The host's stalls: RAR-S128's 60,030 games hold 66 sides over 20 ms in
  nine bursts, 24 over 45 ms and 19 over 54 ms, the largest 178 ms. Five
  bursts are three hours apart to the minute (a periodic task), and one
  held twelve slots at once.
- A stall forfeits only where it lands on a low-slack move (1.85% of the
  kernel's moves have under 75 ms, 5.9% under 100 ms): about 0.5 to 1.5
  forfeits over a run to the ceiling, order of magnitude only. RAR-R14
  recorded four such forfeits in 11,200 games at a similar floor, which
  says the estimate may be low.

**So the tune runs on the runner's own allowance** (the larger of 3 and
0.5% of the games so far). A forfeit is scored as the loss it is, the
reader reads FAULT at the next checkpoint, and the tune stops there for
the maintainer (rule 7c). *Acceptance, registered in advance:* a time loss
whose `failed-games` table shows the losing search held longer than its
slack is the host's and is accepted; a search that overran its own
deadline with no hold is the engine's and returns D.7 to the clock.

### Pause and resume

There is a clean one, and the wrapper lacks nothing.

- One interrupt asks the runner for a clean stop: no new game starts, games
  in flight get 30 seconds (`--stop-grace-secs`), and only whole iterations
  count, so the iteration in flight is replayed.
- The same command resumes. The run directory holds the seed, the
  schedule, the stored horizon and the checkpoints; the wrapper carries the
  seed, checks every guard again and plays on to the next checkpoint. The
  perturbation signs come from the seed and the iteration number, so the
  resumed run is the registered one.
- It has been done: RAR-S128 was interrupted at iteration 2,001 (its
  manifest's `interrupted` lines), and RAR-R34's `10+0.1` read resumed from
  its checkpoint after a console died.
- *Its test on this build:* the pilot is interrupted once and run again;
  the reader's move check on the resumed run (worst error under 1e-6) is
  the proof.

### Reads

A read plays the tune build on both sides, the rounded centres at a
completed iteration against the start, values taken from `spsa history`
without writing into the run directory. It shares no host with a running
tune: its wrapper refuses a busy host.

| Read | When | Games | Its use | Rule |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| At the stop | after the tune has ended; no conflict | 2,000, about 27 minutes | the only measurement of this fit's own contribution | theta is baked unless the interval lies wholly below zero |
| Interim | optional, the maintainer's, with the tune paused, or played after the stop from the journal | 1,000, about 14 minutes | an alarm for gross harm, sharper than the tune's own gain read (SE 6.7 Elo against a null SD near 14) | wholly below zero is reported; the maintainer may then place `STOP` |

- *Sizing.* At 2,000 games "wholly below zero" catches a true −15 Elo nine
  times in ten and a true −10 a little over half; the predicted +8 crosses
  it under once in a thousand. "Baked" therefore means no harm beyond about
  −15 shown. At 1,000 games the alarm catches −20 at 85%.
- *What a read cannot tell:* whether more iterations will gain, which
  coordinates carry a gain, or the gain at `10+0.1`. **No forecast is made
  from a read**, two reads never rank two checkpoints, and theta is never
  chosen by a read.
- *Why the read at the stop:* D.7.13's SPRT against the classic would pass
  on the pre-fit kernel (RAR-S98 and RAR-S99 reached H1 in 1,051 and 875
  pairs on +35.5 and +42.3 Elo; +63.8 is about 600). A fit that cost 10 Elo
  would pass with it.
- *Not registered:* a `10+0.1` read of theta against the start. No rule
  consumes it; D.7.13's companion reads the fitted kernel there.

## 5. The surrounding steps

PLAN D.7.11 holds them in order. In short:

1. *The tune build* (`I1`): seven seeds added, `q_see_correction_scale` an
   option; the fingerprint unchanged.
2. *The surface files and the activation read* (`V`): the seven new options
   active, the rest as read here; the coverage audit clean.
3. *The registration* (`M`), RAR-S135, before any game: this record's
   surface, shape, fault rule, reads and prediction.
4. *The drivers and dry runs* (`V`): one command each for the pilot, the
   tune and a read, each run once through its dry path.
5. *The pilot* (the maintainer's): **64 iterations**, about 26 minutes, not
   128. Its uses are the run proved end to end, the rate, the spread of an
   iteration's score at the first perturbations, and the pause; each is
   settled inside 64. Pass: every option accepted, no first arm clamped, no
   fault, the move check after one interrupt, the rate recorded. Its theta
   is never used.
6. *The run* (the maintainer's).
7. *The read at the stop* (`V`).
8. *The bake* (`I1`): theta from `theta-<K>.toml` by script,
   `tools/spsa_theta_audit.py` first, the tune build at its defaults and
   the default build both reproducing the tune binary's `bench 13` at
   theta.

**D.7.12** then fits `a(m)` on the read at the stop's games, theta's side
only (about 130,000 moves), or on a self-play match of the baked kernel.
**D.7.13** needs the baked PGO build and its fingerprint; its SPRT decides
adoption and its two companions carry the information.

### The inactive seeds are not removed now

**I recommend against removing any of them before D.7.14.**

- *What the evidence is:* eight seeds change no decision on forty positions
  at depth 13 or 17. That is not a count of how often their terms act in
  games, and `KPickCheckPiece` shows the difference: inactive at 13, active
  at 17.
- *What each is:* `rfp_min_16` and `nmp_min_16` are floors under two
  margins; `window_stability_cap` caps a count; `tt_cut_fifty` is a rule of
  the fifty-move clock, which no bench position approaches;
  `pick_check_base` reads as an offset common to every evasion;
  `fds_singular` and its two neighbours are the one term whose reach is not
  known.
- *Why not now:* a removal moves the fit's baseline, D.7.11.1 must
