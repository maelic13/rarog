## 1. Findings, in order of consequence

1. **The candidate is a large, real improvement and should continue.** +52.16
   ± 10.73 Elo at equal time, unfitted, with no forfeit or crash in 2,000
   games, on the harness that measured 2.4.0's whole release at +55. The
   gain sits in the middlegame and early endgame (section 3): the candidate
   converts a piece-up advantage twice as reliably as the B.1 head in the
   same games (27 thrown away against 54) and wins its decisive games sooner
   than it loses them. That is the signature of the evaluation-accuracy and
   ordering work the cluster landed, not of tactics, which is why the
   tactical screens did not see it.
2. **"0.683x NPS" is not a 32% loss of work; about 13 points of it are a
   different tree and about 20 points are dearer nodes, and no single item
   is the cause.** Per interior node the candidate does *less*: 22% fewer
   moves made, 84% fewer check tests, 34% fewer quiescence nodes, the same
   number of table probes (section 2.1). Its nodes are dearer because five
   or six new per-node items each cost 15–50 ns: richer quiet scoring and
   selection, SEE on quiets, threats and check masks, correction reads,
   history updates at every node. The one big suspect a cache-footprint
   argument names, the 25 MiB of correction tables, is refuted: sixteen
   times smaller keyed tables change NPS by 0.0% (section 2.2). The
   candidate reaches bench depth 13 in 2.16 s against B.1's 2.38 s, and at
   the clock it reaches the same mean depth. The registered NPS floor was
   the wrong instrument for a tree-shape change; B.7's recoverable share is
   perhaps 5–8% NPS, not 32%.
3. **The implementation is correct against the donors on every mechanism
   the results made me re-read** (section 4), with three deliberate
   departures already on record (no full-depth-search branch, no reduction
   at the root, in check or of the first move) and **two admission choices
   that follow the donors and depart from the B.1 head, both measurable:**
   the correction tables train on mate-range residuals and at
   singular-excluded nodes. Mate-range residuals are the whole of the
   "825 cp mean residual" anomaly (61 cp without them), and each admission
   shrinks the bench tree by 15–20% (section 2.3). The donors do both with
   NNUE; whether an HCE benefits is a 20-minute paired run each, and the
   answer is categorical, so it belongs before SPSA.
4. **Razoring is the one mechanism the fixed-node screens convict, and the
   conviction is specific.** Removing it lifts the canaries 75 → 104 and WAC
   at 100k 204 → 217 while costing 28% more nodes to depth 13. Two guards
   changed at once: the B.1 head razored only off PV lines (`!tt_pv`) and
   only at depth ≤ 3; the candidate razors on stored-PV nodes and at any
   depth, the donor's shape, whose margins encode an evaluation Rarog's
   does not have (the classical oracle razors at depth 1 only). SPSA can
   move the margins; it cannot restore a guard. One registered paired run
   of the candidate against itself with `!tt_pv` and depth ≤ 3 restored
   decides it (section 5).
5. **Which rung governs: the paired run.** The ladder's floors were
   registered as "ablate below", the ablation ran, and the paired run
   cleared its target. A zero-game screen that a +52 Elo candidate fails is
   a screen measuring something other than strength, and the honest
   conclusion is to fix the screens for B.3–B.5, not to hold the candidate
   (section 6). B.2.3 proceeds once the three categorical questions above
   are answered in games, because SPSA fits around whatever is categorical.
6. **One registered B.2.2 item was not run:** the zero-game curvature sweep
   of five coordinates that conditions SPSA (§9, PLAN rule 4, RAR-S73). It
   is cheap and it is the thing that says whether SPSA has anything to
   find; it runs after the A/Bs settle the arm (section 7).

## 2. Measurements

### 2.1 Per-node work, both arms at stride 1 (`bench 13`, `diag` builds)

Interior nodes 2,766,067 (B.1) against 2,285,006 (candidate); quiescence
nodes 2,820,172 against 1,525,870. Per interior node:

| Work item | B.1 | Candidate | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| moves made (with-check + plain) | 1.81 | 1.46 | 0.80 |
| `gives_check` fast tests | 9.23 | 1.50 | 0.16 |
| quiescence nodes | 1.02 | 0.67 | 0.65 |
| table probes at interior nodes | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| capture generations (staged + full) | 0.47 | 0.54 | 1.13 |
| quiets generated (staged + full) | 5.54 | 6.85 | 1.24 |
| quiets actually searched | 3.09 | 1.36 | 0.44 |
| SEE calls (captures + quiets) | 2.75 | 3.04 | 1.11 |
| `check_info` builds | 0.81 | 0.89 | 1.09 |
| correction updates | 0.12 | 0.10 | 0.78 |
| interior/quiescence hit rates | 62% / 37% | 62% / 38% | — |

Evaluations per node are the same on both arms (a miss evaluates on both;
hit rates are equal), so the evaluation's 29.5% of the candidate's samples is
the HCE's ordinary share, not a new cost. Time per node: B.1 426 ns over
5.59 M nodes, candidate 567 ns over 3.81 M (1.33x). With quiescence nodes
costed at 120–150 ns on both arms, the interior node's non-evaluation cost
rises from about 450–480 ns to 570–590 ns, **+20–25%**, and the remaining
gap to 1.33x is the mix: 60% of the candidate's nodes are interior against
50% of B.1's. The profile's per-item shares (picker 13.4%, SEE 7.3%,
correction 4.1%, history 2.9%, threats 1.6%, check masks 0.9%) are each
small and together are the 100–140 ns.

### 2.2 The footprint hypothesis, refuted

The candidate's keyed correction tables are 16 buckets × 2 × 65,536 × 2 B =
4 MiB each, four of them, against B.1's 256 KiB each; with the two 4.5 MiB
continuation-correction tables the per-thread footprint went from about
1 MiB to 25 MiB, randomly addressed six times per node. A worktree build
with `KEY_SLOTS` 65,536 → 4,096 (keyed tables 16x smaller, fingerprint
5,746,165) benched interleaved five times against the candidate: **ratio of
medians 1.000** (2,007,043 against 2,006,355 nodes per second). The
footprint is not the cost. Reckless carries the same tables (its
`CorrectionHistory` is `[16][2][65536]`) and maps its continuation tables
with huge pages on Linux; on this host the TLB argument does not survive the
measurement, so no large-page or prefetch ticket is warranted.

### 2.3 Correction-training admission, two variants

The candidate's mean admitted residual is 825 cp (179,956,255 over 218,190
updates) against B.1's 128. B.1 refused two classes the candidate admits:
mate-range scores (`|score| < MATE − MAX_PLY`) and singular-excluded nodes
(`excluded.is_null()`). Both donors admit both (Reckless's tail guard has
neither test; Stockfish's `(bestValue > staticEval) == bool(bestMove)` has
neither). Two `b2core,diag` worktree builds, one guard each:

| Arm | bench 13 | interior / quiescence | updates | mean residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| candidate | 4,706,910 | 2,285,006 / 1,525,870 | 218,190 | 825 |
| a: no training at excluded nodes | 5,686,019 (+21%) | 2,788,983 / 1,832,779 | 228,167 | 1,140 |
| b: no decisive residuals | 5,428,512 (+15%) | 2,627,891 / 1,773,240 | 238,217 | **61** |

Reading: the large residuals are mates, not singular nodes (a's mean rises
because it removes small residuals and keeps the mates). Each admission
shrinks the tree by a sixth: a mate found below a quiet position clamps a
full-strength update into four tables every time, and those corrections
then widen the pruning margins through the `|corr|` terms and shrink
reductions, which is P6's warning about a correction consumer acting as a
pruning signal. Neither variant is a defect against the handoff; both are
categorical; both are 20-minute paired runs.

### 2.4 Where the +52 comes from (the paired PGN)

Candidate score by game length: 0–60 plies 53.5% (130 games), 60–100
**62.1%** (427), 100–140 **58.9%** (747), 140–200 54.7% (553), 200+ 50.3%
(143). Every decisive game ends in mate (no adjudication); the candidate's
wins have median length 127 plies, its losses 139. Conversion audit at the
registered parameters over the same 2,000 games: candidate draws-up 27
(22 in an endgame), losses-up 0, thrown away **27**; B.1 draws-up 47,
losses-up 7, thrown away **54**; saved from a persistent deficit, candidate
47 draws + 7 wins, B.1 27 + 0. Median draw 102 plies.

## 3. Why the screens missed it

WAC at fixed nodes, the oracle's best-move agreement and the canaries all
measure tactical resolution per node against a Stockfish-classical
reference; the equal-time WAC proxy reads the candidate at 190 against 200.
The games say the candidate is 52 Elo stronger. Both are true: the cluster
bought positional accuracy (correction, threat-aware ordering, history
policy) and conversion, and paid for it with a little tactical depth per
node (razoring on stored-PV nodes, section 4). The screens were chosen from
the oracle's strengths. For B.3–B.5 the positional half needs an
instrument: a fixed-time positional set (STS, 1,500 positions, public) run
by `fixed_budget_probe.py` at a node budget, reported beside WAC, and the
paired run kept as the only strength rung.

## 4. Correctness against the donors

Re-read with the results in hand, formula by formula against Reckless's
source (lines cited in the evidence directory) and Stockfish `59aae690`:

- **Move-loop pruning**: LMP `(2818 + 78·improvement/16 + 1351·d² +
  74·history/1024)/1024`, quiet futility with the fail-soft raise and
  `depth < 14`, bad-noisy futility at the BadNoisy stage with `break`,
  history pruning `< −948·d` at `depth < 5`, SEE both branches with the
  handoff's ×0.75 seeds: **identical to Reckless**, with the composite
  history `quiet + cont1 + cont2` and `eval` the corrected static eval as
  the donor uses.
- **LMR**: every term and its sign checked against Reckless's block
  (`269·ilog2`, improvement clamp (−241, 1155), correction, exact, TT-score
  and TT-depth, `is_win(beta)`, quiet and noisy bases with their history
  terms, `alpha − est` clamp, critical-ply, PV window term, laterality,
  `tt_pv` three, cut-node two, gives-check, cutoff-count two, parent, the
  jitter), `reduced_depth` floor and cap, the re-search rule with the
  converted 26/4 thresholds, `last_critical_ply` propagation: **identical**.
  Stockfish's floor is the same shape (`max(1, min(newDepth − r/1024,
  newDepth + 2)) + PvNode`).
- **Node entry**: eval-difference history training, hindsight, the
  estimated-score refinement (`Exact` always, bounds when they tighten),
  the eval store on a miss with `tt_pv`: identical.
- **Razoring**: Reckless `!PV && est < alpha − 237 − 254·d² && alpha < 2048
  && !tt_move.is_quiet() && bound != Lower`, no depth cap, no `tt_pv` guard;
  the candidate matches with converted margins. Stockfish dev is now linear
  (`eval < alpha − 482·depth`, `!PvNode`), the classical oracle razors at
  depth 1 only with margin 527. **The B.1 head had `!tt_pv` and depth ≤ 3.**
  This is the mechanism the ablation convicts.
- **Correction admission**: section 2.3; donor-faithful, B.1-divergent.
- **Deviations on record** (B.2.1 review §3): no full-depth-search branch
  (the donor reduces a non-PV node's first move by one or two plies from its
  own thresholds), no reduction at the root or in check. The candidate's
  interior-node count at depth 8 is 2.30x the oracle's against B.1's 1.80x;
  the absent branch is the most likely reason, and it is a registered switch
  worth one paired run after the three above.
- **Unit-scale notes for SPSA**: three hard-coded clamps are in the donor's
  evaluation units and were not converted: the LMR improvement clamp
  (−241, 1155), the `alpha − est` clamp (−65, 91), and the hindsight-deepen
  threshold is a coordinate but its reduce margin 26 is converted. SPSA
  scales the coefficients around them; converting the clamps (×0.457) is a
  one-line ticket before SPSA so the fitted coefficients mean what the
  donor's do.

## 5. Razoring: what to run

Candidate against candidate-with-guards, 2,000 games paired at the
registered settings, guards = B.1's (`!tt_pv`, depth ≤ 3) on the candidate's
margins. Prediction, frozen here: **+8 ± 11 Elo** for the guarded arm; a
result below 0 says the tactical loss is paid for by the node savings and
the guards stay off. If the guarded arm wins, the guard is categorical and
lands before SPSA; the margins remain SPSA coordinates either way. The
oracle's depth-1-only form is the fallback experiment if the first is
inconclusive.

## 6. The ladder and the registrations for B.3–B.5

Ruling recommended: **the paired run governs; zero-game floors are
diagnostics.** A floor failure triggers the ablation and a written cause in
the leaf's record; it never holds a candidate the paired run has cleared,
and it never accepts one the paired run has failed. Registrations for
B.3–B.5 should say so, and should also: register the ablation order (the
eight bits in mechanism order, one sweep); replace the pooled-NPS floor with
time-to-depth on `bench` and the pooled NPS reported as a diagnostic (a
tree-shape change makes nodes incomparable); add a positional fixed-node
screen (STS) beside WAC; keep the canaries as a regression rule (no canary
the baseline solves may be lost) rather than "all pass"; and list the
curvature sweep as a checklist item with its own evidence path. The
`[0,10]` gate stays as registered: bounds do not move after games, and a
candidate this far above the bracket resolves it quickly.

## 7. What precedes B.2.3, in order

1. **Three categorical paired runs, 2,000 games each, maintainer-run,
   registered as one bounded screen** (AGENTS permits a baseline/A/B screen
   that cheaply separates interaction): (a) razoring guards restored;
   (b) no decisive residuals in correction training; (c) no correction
   training at singular-excluded nodes. Each arm is one `CoreParams` switch
   or one guard behind a test, fingerprinted, PGO-built by the implementer.
   Predictions frozen in the registration; each adopted only if it measures
   ≥ +5 with the interval excluding −5.
2. **Optional fourth run**: the donor's full-depth-search branch as a switch
   (reduces a non-PV node's first move by 1–2 plies from its own
   thresholds), since it is the largest remaining donor shape not built and
   the interior-node excess points at it. Same rule.
3. **Convert the three eval-unit clamps** (section 4), fingerprint-changing,
   in the same commit series, before the sweep.
4. **The curvature sweep** (§9's five coordinates, 0.5x–2x, bench and WAC at
   100k) on the arm the runs leave; it is the registered condition for SPSA.
5. **B.7's behaviour-neutral micro-tickets**, fingerprint-identical, any
   time: SEE early exit for a quiet whose destination the threat set does
   not attack; one `check_info` per node shared by picker and pruning;
   `threats()` skipped at a quiescence node with no captures; the picker's
   post-skip survivor scan without the selection pass. Expected 5–8% NPS.
   Behaviour-changing speed work (table sizes, lazy quiet scoring) waits
   for the accepted head.
6. **B.2.3 SPSA** on the resulting arm; then **B.2.4** at `[0,10]`.

## 8. Predictions, read now (calibration is appended at B.2.4)

P1 missed in sign and magnitude: the prior "converted seeds are roughly
neutral" undervalued the correction and ordering work. P3 missed in
direction because the LMR floor clamps 34% of reductions (`lmr_floor_hits`
0.34 per node): the donor's reduction sum is large at Rarog's history scale,
so the floor holds many moves at one ply where B.1 reduced them into
quiescence, and the branching factor falls rather than rises. P4 stopped
short for the same reason. P5's mechanism was wrong (threats are 1.6% of
samples) and its instrument was wrong (NPS across tree shapes). P6 is
partly answered by section 2.3: the correction is acting as a pruning
signal through the mate-range residuals, and the bench moves 15% when they
are refused; the shadow-first admission profile was not recorded and
should be, once, on the arm that goes to SPSA.
