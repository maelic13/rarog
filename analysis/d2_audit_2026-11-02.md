# D.2 audit, 2026-11-02: the four-thread card's evidence re-read

An independent re-read of PLAN D.2, its experiment records (RAR-M74 to
RAR-M80, RAR-P46 to RAR-P53, RAR-R29 to RAR-R33), the Basilisk cross-check's
placements and the raw runs behind them, asked for by the maintainer when the
leaf stopped progressing. Nothing was built and no game was played; one
engine output check of a few milliseconds confirmed the `info` line format.
The scripts and logs are in `analysis/artifacts/d2-audit/` (ignored):
`pgn_side_depth.py` (`4f08cfec…`), `replay_fidelity_nonmate.py`
(`d297c9dc…`), `pgn_side_depth.log` (`76021f84…`),
`replay_fidelity_nonmate.log` (`f944bf02…`).

## 2. RAR-M78's verdict falls with its confounded leg

RAR-M79 showed the same configuration at R 3.44 where RAR-M78's leg ran at
2.48, and recorded the leg as confounded; the audit agrees. What follows was
not drawn: the deficit verdict rested on that leg alone, so **"Rarog
deficient" is not established**, and the card's research question is open
again rather than answered.

Frozen prediction for a clean rerun of the threads leg at R about 3.4, from
the odds leg's 101.8 Elo a doubling: the four-thread side gains
ln(3.44 / 2.48) / ln 2 = 0.47 doublings over the confounded leg, so **G4
+122 ± 12, D 1.2, X 2.3**, level with Reckless (2.30) and inside Stockfish's
error (2.48). G4 at about +85 or below reads deficient against Reckless
again (the registered rule's upper bound), and then the lead of section 4 is
the engine's own target. Between those the read is unresolved and a second
night decides.

## 3. The host state is the blocker, and it is unidentified

What is established:

- Four-thread processes alone lose about a third of their speed, flat for
  hours, and no one-thread side ever did: RAR-M74 (2.60 against B.9's 3.72
  on another day), RAR-P46 (5 of 12 passes, 100 ms searches), RAR-M78 (a
  whole 1,000-game leg at 2.48, flat from the first game), RAR-M79 `solo`
  (2.69, fifty minutes after `on` read 3.44). Healthy: 3.44 to 3.53 in games
  (RAR-M79), 3.57 to 3.66 pinned alone (RAR-R29, RAR-P52), 3.78 alone in the
  slot (RAR-R31).
- The head's processes only, so far. B.9 read 3.28 and 3.72 on two days, and
  Reckless and Stockfish 3.45 to 3.47 in one session each; none has been
  seen in a slow session, but none has been exposed to one either.
- RAR-M79's `off` leg ran in the healthy state, so it cannot clear large
  pages for the slow one (the record and D.7 said it did; corrected).
- In the slow state the main thread loses about twice the depth its NPS loss
  predicts: RAR-M78 1.05 plies against about 0.6 expected (0.35 from the
  speed, 0.15 from X, 0.09 from the own time), `solo` 0.93 against about 0.5.
  A uniform slowdown of every thread would not do that; the main thread is
  hit harder than the average, or intermittently.
- The host runs a custom power plan ("ChrisTitus - Ultimate Power Plan",
  minimum and maximum processor state 100%); its core-parking and boost
  settings are at their defaults in the registry, which the audit did not
  resolve to values.

Candidates that predict R near 2.5 and a one-thread side untouched: two
threads sharing a physical core (the slot is eight logical CPUs on four
cores; a parked core or a scheduler state packs the threads), and a parked or
down-clocked core under the four-thread process. Neither has been observed;
every read so far measured R and inferred.

The instrument is passive and costs nothing: `typeperf` at one-second
intervals over the slot's logical CPUs (`\Processor Information(n,m)\%
Processor Time`, `Parking Status`, `% of Maximum Frequency`, `Processor
Frequency`; all exist on this host) and `\Thread(rarog*)\% Processor Time`,
written to a CSV beside the run's manifest, from now on beside every
four-thread leg. The next slow episode then names its mechanism instead of
costing another leg. With it, each leg's per-side in-game R becomes its
validity check: a four-thread Rarog block under 3.0 (Reckless under 3.2) is
recorded and not read.

## 4. The engine-side lead that stands

- Four threads reach a fixed depth with about four times one thread's nodes
  and more slowly than one (RAR-R29, X 0.87 at the head, 0.96 at B.9); the
  helpers' entries reach the main thread (+5.6 points of hits) and do not
  cut its tree (RAR-R33, M 1.09). In games Reckless's and Stockfish's
  four-thread sides reach their one-thread depth at about 1.3 to 1.6 times
  its nodes (section 1's node bins), so their helpers feed the main thread's
  depth and Rarog's do not.
- It predates Phase C, so it is not the C head's regression, and it is
  priced only by the rerun of section 2: Lazy SMP's gain can be width alone,
  and only games say what Rarog's width is worth.
- The mate-score behaviour at four threads (a stop at 7 to 10 plies where
  one thread iterates on) is unexamined; it is H5's domain and decided
  positions, so it waits.

## 9. After the night of 2026-11-02/03 (RAR-M80, RAR-M81): section 3 corrected

The night read Rarog's four threads at 2.62 to 2.66 times one thread for
9.5 hours in every leg, and Reckless's at 3.36 to 3.48 beside them on the
same quiet host, with no core parked and every logical CPU at full
frequency. Section 3 called the slow state the host's; it is Rarog's, and it
is the state of every Colosseum game Rarog has played at four threads but
RAR-M79's fast fifths. From the sampler's thread CSVs
(`analysis/artifacts/d2-audit/idle_side_cpu.py`, `e7ca1598…`;
`idle_side_cpu.log`, `0caf7ae3…`; the collector's wrapped samples dropped):

- On the samples that lie inside its own turn (summed CPU at least 350%),
  each of Rarog's four search threads runs at 94 to 95% of a logical CPU, in
  all three slots, exactly as Reckless's (95 to 96%). The threads are fully
  scheduled: the loss is not a thread waiting.
- The one-thread sides never exceed 101% and sit at 50% over the leg (their
  half of the turns), for both engines: no idle process burns a core.
- So each Rarog thread, while running, produces about two thirds of what it
  produces alone (7.37 M over four against 2.78 M for one; alone in the same
  slot, RAR-R31, 11.1 M over four), where Reckless's produce 0.86 of their
  single thread. A thread that is scheduled the whole second and does two
  thirds of its work is sharing its physical core: two of Rarog's threads on
  one core's two logical CPUs each yield about 0.6 to 0.65, and four threads
  on two cores give R near 2.6, which is what every slow leg reads. The
  sampler's per-slot pairing (Rarog's load on both siblings of a core more
  often than Reckless's) points the same way without proving it.

The mechanism to test is therefore Windows' placement of Rarog's threads
inside the slot's eight logical CPUs, not parking, frequency, scheduling
share, the opponent's process or the host. PLAN D.2's next read stands and
its prediction sharpens: Rarog's four threads confined to one logical CPU
per physical core restore R near 3.8 (0.75); the same four on both siblings
of two cores read near 2.6; Reckless reads the same either way. If so, the
repair is the engine's: each search thread given its own physical core
inside the process's affinity mask (an ideal processor per thread, or a
helper placement option), behaviour-neutral, fingerprint exact, closed by
the in-game R and a four-thread read, and worth about 0.4 of a doubling at
four threads in every harness that confines an engine to a core group.

Rarog's four threads sat on both siblings of two physical cores in 90-96% of
their turns, all night, in every slot, with the slot's other two cores
nearly idle. That is the two-threads-per-core state section 9 inferred, now
observed. Reckless's four were packed too, on two cores in half to two
thirds of its turns and on three in most of the rest, one per core almost
never. So Windows 11 does not spread four busy threads over the four cores
of an eight-logical-CPU affinity mask for either engine; it packs them, and
it packed Rarog's harder. Neither engine places its own threads: Reckless's
`numa.rs` binds a thread to a NUMA node only on Linux and only when the
process mask spans more than one node; on Windows it spawns plain threads,
as Rarog does. The ratio difference (2.65 against 3.46) is the packing
difference and whatever each engine loses per shared core, not a mechanism
in Rarog's code.

Two corrections to section 9 follow. The slow state is not "Rarog's, not the
host's": it is the scheduler's placement inside Colosseum's slot, which hits
both engines, Rarog more. And the repair is not first the engine's. The fair
measurement is a slot of one logical CPU per physical core per side, which
the pinned Colosseum (0.5.0) can already give: `plan_cpu_placement` accepts
an explicit logical-CPU list that "may name a subset of a physical core"
(`crates/colosseum-engine/src/placement.rs`), so a run passing the even
logical CPUs of its game cores needs no engine change and no new runner
feature. The standalone reads that gave 3.6-3.8 were taken on
`Get-HarnessGameCpus`, which is exactly that set (physical cores, CPU 0
excluded). Hard affinity inside the engine stays off the table: it would
fight the tester's mask and any other engine on the box, and no donor does
it (Stockfish binds only when the process mask is the whole machine and
spans NUMA nodes). A soft ideal-processor hint per search thread, inside the
inherited mask, is the only engine-side form worth considering, and only
once it is known how CCRL's testers place a four-thread engine, which this
machine cannot tell.

PLAN D.2's next read therefore becomes a zero-game placement read with no
rebuilt slot and no engine arm: Rarog 4T against 1T and Reckless 4T against
1T with `--placement` naming one logical CPU per game core, the in-game R
per fifth as the instrument, on an idle host. Prediction frozen here: Rarog
reads R at or above 3.5 in every fifth (0.75); Reckless at or above 3.5
(0.8). If both hold, RAR-M81's "deficient" Elo verdict was measured in a
packed state and the self-relative comparison is re-registered in the
sibling-free slot; the engine-side hint is a separate leaf, held on the
CCRL-placement question.
