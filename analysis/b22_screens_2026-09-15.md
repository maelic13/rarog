# B.2.2 screens — the unfitted `b2core` candidate against B.0's registered numbers

**Status: deliverable of PLAN B.2.2 (class `V`), 2026-09-15, and the brief
for its review. The zero-game screens, the registered 2,000-game paired run
and the ETW profile are all in.** Subject: the `b2core` arm of `dev` at engine commit `1a27889`
(the B.2.1 core plus B.2.0.2's MultiPV guards, which are inert at one line),
4,706,910 / EBF 2.391. Baseline: the B.1 head, whose §11 numbers reproduce the
2.4.0 head's exactly (RAR-P24). Thresholds and predictions:
`analysis/search_programme_2026-09-13.md` §11 and §12, frozen 2026-09-13.
Ledger: RAR-S73. Raw evidence: ignored `tools/results/b22-20260915/` and
`tools/results/nps-b22-core-20260915/`.

## 1. Verdict

**The unfitted candidate plays +52.16 ± 10.73 Elo stronger than the B.1
head in the registered 2,000-game paired run (section 7), while four
registered zero-game floors fail: pooled NPS (0.683x), WAC at 100k nodes,
oracle agreement at 300k, and the canaries.** Branching and WAC at 400k pass
their floors, and branching is inside its window. The paired run is above
its target, which says B.2.3 proceeds directly. The floor rule says ablate
once, then re-plan. The registration has no rule for when the two disagree,
so section 9 puts that to the review. No ablation order was ever registered,
so the one ablation ran as an unordered sweep of all eight bits (section 4).
The profile (section 8) does not support the B.2.1 review's speed suspects.

## 4. One ablation sweep

`b2core,ablate,tune` build (non-PGO; node-limited screens do not depend on
speed), one bit at a time. At mask 0 it reproduces the PGO candidate on
all 300 WAC positions at 100k (move, depth and node count), and on WAC.001's
depth record; mask 128 benches 25,183,900 as B.2.1 recorded.

| Arm | WAC 100k | Agreement 300k | Canaries |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| B.1 head | 200 | 40 | 67 |
| candidate | 204 | 35 | 75 |
| − razoring (1) | **217** | 39 | **104** |
| − reverse futility (2) | 198 | 35 | 77 |
| − null move (4) | 206 | 37 | 73 |
| − ProbCut (8) | 213 | **40** | 78 |
| − IIR and hindsight (16) | 205 | 36 | 77 |
| − move-loop pruning (32) | 160 | 34 | 73 |
| − singular (64) | 210 | 39 | 70 |
| − LMR (128) | 204 | 37 | 77 |

**Razoring is the outlier.** Removing it alone raises the canaries from 75
to 104 and WAC from 204 to 217, above the floor, and brings agreement to 39.
ProbCut and singular are next and far smaller on the canaries. Every other
bit leaves the canaries within ±4. This locates where the re-plan should look
first; it is not evidence that removing razoring gains strength (PLAN rule 8),
and at fixed nodes an ablated arm spends its nodes elsewhere, so a bit's row
mixes the mechanism's error with its node savings.

## 6. Speed against quality, before the games

The candidate reaches the bench's nominal depth sooner: `bench 13` takes
4,706,910 / 2,183,674 = 2.16 s against 7,601,220 / 3,199,166 = 2.38 s for
B.1 (0.91x, from the interleaved pooled medians). An equal-time proxy on WAC
ran the candidate at 0.683 of each budget: **190 at 68,300 nodes against
B.1's 200 at 100k; 235 at 273,200 against B.1's 237 at 400k.** Before the
games, this record read that as "the better node choice does not yet pay for
the per-node cost" and recommended against B.2.3 on this arm. **Section 7
contradicts that reading, and the recommendation is withdrawn.**

**The B.2.1 review's suspects are small.** `threats()` and `check_info`
together are about 2.5% of samples, so they cannot explain a 32% NPS loss.
The visible costs are the evaluation, TT probing, quiet-move scoring and
selection in the picker, and correction lookups. **Limitation:** there is no
matching profile of the off arm, so this says where the candidate's time
goes, not what grew. Two facts point to node mix as well as per-node cost.
At depth 8 the candidate searches more interior nodes and fewer quiescence
nodes than B.1 (section 5). And at `bench 13` it searches 2,285,006 interior
against 2,766,067, with 1,525,870 quiescence nodes against 2,820,172. A
full-search node evaluates, probes, scores and orders; a quiescence node
does less. B.7's ticket needs the off-arm profile before it names a cost.

## 9. For the review

The record so far, stated plainly: an unfitted, donor-shaped selectivity core
that the B.2.1 review accepted as correct measures **+52 ± 11 Elo** against
its baseline at `3+0.03`. It is **31.7% slower** per node, and it **fails
four zero-game screens** that were registered to catch a weak candidate
before it spent games.

Questions the implementer cannot settle alone:

1. **Which rung governs?** The paired run clears its target while the
   zero-game floors fail. Does B.2.3's SPSA proceed on this arm, or does the
   floor rule's "ablate once, re-plan" come first? If the floors stay, what
   do they now protect against, given that they would have stopped a +52 Elo
   candidate?
2. **Are the screens measuring the wrong thing?** WAC at fixed nodes, oracle
   best-move agreement and the canaries all read the candidate as roughly B.1
   level or worse; the games do not. Candidate explanations to rule on:
   (a) tactical-suite quality at fixed nodes is a weak proxy for game
   strength once the tree shape changes; (b) the oracle-anchored canaries
   encode the oracle's pruning preferences (classical Stockfish with Rarog's
   evaluation) rather than correctness; (c) the gain is positional or
   endgame, which WAC does not sample; (d) the time manager interacts with a
   different tree, so the candidate spends its time differently at a clock.
   Which cheap measurement discriminates them? One candidate: the phase-4
   suite or a positional set at fixed time rather than fixed nodes.
3. **Is razoring a defect or a trade?** Removing it lifts the canaries
   75 → 104 and WAC 204 → 217 at fixed nodes (section 4). Given section 7,
   is that a measured tactical blind spot worth a registered ablation in
   games, or an artefact of the same proxy problem? The seeds came from the
   0.457 scale conversion (B.0 §8).
4. **Speed.** The profile does not support the review's `threats()`
   hypothesis. What is the off-arm comparison B.7 needs, and should B.7's
   speed pass come before B.2.3's tune, or after the gate, given that a
   faster candidate changes what SPSA fits?
5. **Registration hygiene.** The PLAN leaf promised an ablation "in a
   registered order" that no document registered, and a floor/target ladder
   with no rule for disagreement. What should B.3–B.5's screen registrations
   say differently?
6. **Predictions.** P1 missed in sign, P3 in direction, P4 in magnitude and
   P5 badly. Before B.2.3 or B.2.4, should P2 (+35 [+5, +70] after fitting)
   be read as already largely met unfitted, and what does that imply for the
   `[0,10]` gate's bounds, which were chosen for a smaller expected effect?
   The bounds must not change after games are seen. The question is only
   whether an additional registration is warranted.

What is not in question: the match's provenance (section 7), the pooled NPS
measurement (0.683x, three distinct PGO builds per arm, interleaved, host
idle), and that every screen was run to its registered recipe. Two deviations
are recorded: the unordered ablation sweep in place of a registered order,
and the WAC.001 depth record taken at `depthpv 14` so that the ≤ 11
requirement could be read.
