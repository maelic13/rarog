## Experiment and conditions

**A.8.2 four-thread pool against the four targets and Basilisk - REGISTERED, NOT YET RUN.** Same pool settings at `Threads=4`, no `-use-affinity`, concurrency chosen so that four times the concurrency stays at or below 14 cores, run only after a 4T null pair (the same Rarog binary on both sides, 400 games) reads inside +/-10 Elo. Opponents: Critter 1.6a, Houdini 3, Rybka 4, Fritz 16, Basilisk **1.10.0** (substituted for 1.9.3 on 2026-09-11), **Rarog 2.3.2 and Rybka 3** (added 2026-09-11, decision (5)); 400 games each. **NULL RESIZED AND HARNESS RESOLVED - maintainer decision 2026-09-11, before any game. The `+/-10 Elo` null pair above is withdrawn and replaced.** *Why it was withdrawn:* it could not be run as written. `sprt.ps1 -Mode calibrate` bounds a null in **nElo**, the row asks for **Elo**, and 400 games delivers neither - a +/-10 nElo null needs roughly 4,600 games, about **4h05m** at 4T. It was also disproportionate: RAR-M46's own per-pair resolution is **+/-25 Elo at 400 games**, so a +/-10 nElo null would be more precise than the experiment it guards, spending four hours to exclude a bias too small to change any conclusion this row draws. *Second defect, found 2026-09-11:* the row says `no -use-affinity`, which is **fastchess vocabulary in a run that happens in Colosseum**, the harness RAR-M45 used and the one 'the same pool at 4T' means. Colosseum exposes no affinity flag; the clause is inapplicable and is struck, replaced by the core budget below. **Configuration, fixed now:** Colosseum gauntlet, Rarog 2.4.0 against Critter 1.6a, Houdini 3, Rybka 4, Fritz 16, Basilisk 1.10.0, Rarog 2.3.2 and Rybka 3, 400 games each = **2,800 games**; `time_control` 3000+30, `games_per_pair` 2, `common.threads` **4**, `common.hash_mb` **512** (raised from 64 on 2026-09-11, see decision (4)), `concurrency` **3** so that 3x4 = 12 stays at or below the 14 physical cores, adjudication fully off (`max_moves`, `draw`, `resign` all null), book `UHO_Lichess_4852_v1.epd`. **The 2026-08-01 4T gauntlet is NOT a config precedent** - it ran `concurrency 4` (16 threads), `hash_mb 256` and adjudication ON, and differs from this row on all three. **REPLACEMENT VALIDATION, two parts.** *V1 - 4T/1T NPS scaling, **zero extra games**, computed from RAR-M46's own games against RAR-M45's 1T per-engine means.* Required: Rarog's ratio **>= 3.0**. Every opponent's ratio is recorded, and **any opponent below 1.5x is reported as a handicap comparison, not an SMP comparison**, because an engine ignoring the thread setting turns the 4T row into 4-threads-against-1. This failure mode is already observed in this harness, not hypothetical: on 2026-08-01 Rarog 2.3.0 scaled 2.61M -> 12.26M while **HIARCS 14 scaled 0.29M -> 0.29M, exactly 1.00x**. *V2 - self-null, WITHDRAWN 2026-09-11 by decision (2) below; retained here as the record of what it would have tested.* Rarog 2.4.0 on both sides, byte-identical binary, the configuration above unchanged. Pass requires all four: **(a)** the 95% Elo interval **contains zero** - a self-pair's true value is exactly 0, so an interval excluding it is a harness finding; **(b)** zero forfeits, zero crashes; **(c)** white-side and black-side mean NPS agree **within 5%**, which is the direct test for asymmetric starvation and is visible at 400 games where Elo is not; **(d)** the stored `config_json` reads back `threads` 4, `concurrency` 3, `hash_mb` 64 and all three adjudication fields null. **The gate is in Elo, computed from the null's own W-D-L in Colosseum** - not nElo, and not through `sprt.ps1 -Mode calibrate`, which removes the unit confusion that created the original defect rather than restating it. **Expected width, stated before the run:** about **+/-23 Elo at 95%**, scaled from the measured Basilisk 1.10.0 versus 1.9.3 pair (+/-19 at 600 games); if the realised half-width exceeds **+/-30 Elo** the null established nothing and must be extended rather than passed. **This is deliberately a gross-error trap, not a calibration.** It cannot see a 10-Elo harness bias and is not intended to; V1 and V2(c) carry the discriminating power and cost nothing, the Elo interval is the backstop. **Total cost: 2,400 games, about 2h08m**, against 8h05m for the run plus the withdrawn null. **THREE MAINTAINER DECISIONS, 2026-09-11, before any game.** **(1) Basilisk 1.10.0 replaces Basilisk 1.9.3** as this row's sibling opponent and as the project's standing Basilisk reference. 1.10.0 is released and frozen; 1.9.3 is superseded. *New prospective prediction for that pair, registered now and not a rewrite of the 2026-09-09 row:* the original reasoning was that the sibling engine's SMP maturity resembles Rarog's, so its 4T gap should track its 1T gap - that reasoning transfers unchanged to 1.10.0, whose 1T number is **-23** (RAR-M45), giving a predicted 4T band of **-43 to -3**. **(2) The self-null (V2) is withdrawn entirely.** It cost 400 games to test four conditions, and **three of the four are recoverable from RAR-M46's own games at zero cost**: forfeits and crashes are counted from the gauntlet's terminations; the `config_json` read-back is stored with the tournament; and the asymmetric-starvation check becomes **per-engine mean NPS as white versus as black, required to agree within 5%**, measured over that engine's own 400 games rather than a separate pair. Only the Elo-interval-contains-zero condition is lost, and it was the weakest of the four - a gross-error trap explicitly recorded as unable to see a 10-Elo bias. **The discriminating power was never in the null**; it is in V1 and in the white/black NPS comparison, both of which survive at zero games. **(3) All ratings are FROZEN for this run and the readout is the performance column.** The maintainer will not let a 4T run update the 1T rating list. Frozen inputs are RAR-M45's fitted values: Houdini 3 **3277**, Critter 1.6a **3197**, Fritz 16 **3165**, Rybka 4 **3102**, Basilisk 1.10.0 **3012**, Rarog 2.4.0 **3003**. *What that buys, registered before exposure:* with every rating fixed, **Rarog's performance rating minus its frozen 3003 is a single-number readout of its SMP quality relative to the field**, because an engine that gains exactly what the field gains from four threads performs at its frozen rating. The 2026-09-09 prediction of 4T deficits 10 to 40 Elo worse therefore restates as a predicted **Rarog performance rating of about 2963 to 2993**. *Two limits recorded now so they are not discovered later:* the frozen ratings are **1T** ratings, so a performance number computed from them is a relative statement about differential scaling and **is not Rarog's 4T rating**; and in a gauntlet each opponent plays only Rarog, so **opponent performance ratings are just the mirror of their head-to-head and carry no independent information**. **The primary readout remains the per-opponent head-to-head Elo**, which is the unit the frozen prediction is written in; the performance column is the aggregate summary. **Revised cost: 2,800 games, about 2h29m** at 18.8 games/min, parallel 3. **(4) HASH RAISED 64 -> 256 MB, maintainer decision 2026-09-11, before any game, and the comparability argument runs the same way.** Measured from RAR-M45's own games: Rarog averages 67.2 moves per side and a 5.02s budget, so **11.2M nodes per side per game at 1T**; its TT holds 6 entries per 64-byte cluster, giving 6.29M entries at 64 MB. That is a **1.8x overwrite at 1T, the pressure the pool actually ran under**. At 4T and 12.26M nps (the rate this harness measured for Rarog 2.3.0 on 2026-08-01) the same game costs **61M nodes per side**, which at 64 MB is a **9.8x overwrite - 5.5 times the 1T pressure**. At 256 MB it is **2.4x** and at **512 MB it is 1.2x**. 512 is chosen: it is the point at which the 4T arm's pressure is below the 1.8x the 1T arm actually ran under, so neither arm can be argued to have been starved relative to the other, and at 128 GB of host RAM the cost of the larger table is nil (three concurrent games, six live engines, 3 GB). **Holding the hash NUMBER constant is what breaks comparability; holding entries-per-node-searched roughly constant is what preserves it.** The mechanism under test is also the reason: lazy SMP cooperates through the TT, so starving the table degrades precisely what this row measures. **CORRECTION, same day, before any game - an earlier draft of this decision recorded the change as 'helping Rarog more than the field' and warned that a good result would be 'partly hash relief'. That framing was wrong and is withdrawn.** There is no engine-neutral hash: these engines search node counts that differ by more than an order of magnitude (Rarog 61M per side per game at 4T against Rybka 4's 2.3M), so every hash value pressures them differently and no value equalises them. **The standard is therefore not neutrality but adequacy - set the hash so that no engine is losing strength to overwrites, then compare.** At 64 MB and 4T, Rarog at 9.8x would have been the MOST starved engine in the field - corrected 2026-09-11 from an earlier draft that said 'the only', which the measurement below does not support. **Measured 1T tree sizes from RAR-M45's own 39,600 games**, nodes per side per game = mean NPS x mean ms/move x mean moves: Houdini 3 **13.5M**, Houdini 1.5a **12.9M**, Critter 1.6a **12.4M**, Basilisk 1.9.3 **12.4M**, Basilisk 1.10.0 **12.3M**, Rarog 2.3.2 **11.3M**, **Rarog 2.4.0 11.3M**, Shredder 12 5.2M, Fritz 16 4.3M, HIARCS 14 1.1M, Rybka 4 0.8M, Rybka 3 0.4M. **Rarog is sixth, not first** - four engines search larger trees than it does at 1T. Scaling each by its measured 4T NPS ratio puts Rarog around 61M and Basilisk 1.10.0 around 58M at 4T, with Houdini 3 near 41M, so several engines would have been starved at 64 MB and Rarog worst, not uniquely. *Density caveat:* node counts are measured, but overwrite ratios divide them by 64 MB at **Rarog's** 6 entries per 64-byte cluster; the closed-source engines' TT geometry is unknown and 10-16 bytes per entry is the usual range, so their ratios carry that uncertainty while the node counts do not. **Removing a handicap is not granting an advantage, and the result must not be discounted as hash relief.** The honest residual, which is small: the 1T arm ran 64 MB at a 1.8x overwrite and this arm runs 512 MB at 1.2x, so the cross-arm comparison is between two adequately-resourced configurations rather than a single-variable contrast. Both sit in the un-starved regime, which is the condition that matters. **Precedent:** the maintainer's own 2026-08-01 pair already did this - hash 64 for the 1T gauntlet and 256 for the 4T gauntlet. *Open question, now partly answered and pointing the other way:* the 1T pool ran 64 MB, where Rarog sits at a **1.8x** overwrite against Houdini 3, Houdini 1.5a, Critter and both Basilisks at **2.0-2.1x**. If 64 MB mildly starved anyone at 1T it starved four of Rarog's opponents slightly MORE than Rarog, so RAR-M45's head-to-heads are more likely to flatter Rarog marginally than to understate it - though every engine in that group sits in a narrow 1.8-2.1x band, so any such effect is small. **Whether 1T Rarog gains from 128 MB (0.89x) is unmeasured and is NOT answerable by bench:** `bench 13` averages about 190k nodes per position over 40 fresh searches, roughly 60x smaller than the 11.3M-node tree a game accumulates across 67 moves, so a bench sweep over Hash would return a confident null that says nothing about game play. The cheap discriminating test is a short self-play pair at Hash 64 against Hash 128, same binary, reading **mean depth** rather than Elo - about 400 games, 5 minutes at parallel 14 - with an Elo test justified only if depth moves. Sizing if it does: resolving +/-3 Elo needs roughly 26,700 games, about 5.4 hours. Unregistered and not scheduled; it would also want deciding before E.2 fixes its gate conditions. **(5) Rarog 2.3.2 may be added as a near-peer opponent, frozen at 2949; its prediction is registered now so it is frozen whether or not it runs.** The field is otherwise entirely stronger than Rarog, which puts every pair in the tails where Elo resolution is worst - RAR-M45 read +/-29 against Houdini 3 against +/-20 for its near-level pairs. 2.3.2 is the only available opponent running **the same lazy SMP code as the candidate**, so it isolates the release from differential scaling. *Prediction:* identical SMP on both sides should leave the 1T gap intact, so **+70 +/- 20** (that is, +50 to +90) against RAR-M45's 1T +70. A 4T result outside that band says the release changed SMP behaviour, which would be a finding in its own right and belongs to D.2. **Rybka 3 is added alongside it, frozen at 2906**, with its own prediction below. **A tension inside the 2026-09-09 prediction, recorded now and deliberately NOT resolved by editing it.** That prediction's stated mechanism is that the opponents' SMP is mature and Rarog's is not. The measured 4T NPS scaling in this harness says the opposite for the oldest engines: Rarog 4.70x and Basilisk 4.68x against **Rybka 4 2.42x, Critter 2.81x, Rybka 3 2.89x, Houdini 1.5a 3.05x**. NPS scaling is not Elo scaling - an engine can convert threads into move quality without converting them into nodes - so this does not refute the prediction, but it does mean the mechanism cannot hold uniformly across a field this wide. *Predictions for the two added opponents, frozen now:* **Rarog 2.3.2 +50 to +90** (identical lazy-SMP code on both sides should leave the 1T +70 intact; a result outside that band says the release changed SMP behaviour, which is a D.2 finding); **Rybka 3 +82 to +120** (its 2.89x scaling is well below Rarog's 4.70x, so the gap should widen in Rarog's favour rather than narrow), confidence low on both. *What the pattern will say:* if the gap improves against Rybka 3 and Rybka 4 while worsening against Houdini 3, Critter and Fritz 16, the 2026-09-09 mechanism was right about the stronger opponents and wrong about the oldest ones, and that split is the result rather than a single averaged number.

## Result / disposition

**Prediction, frozen 2026-09-09:** Rarog's 4T deficits are **10 to 40 Elo worse** than its 1T deficits against the same engines, because the current lazy SMP has never been tuned for quality and the opponents' SMP is mature; Basilisk within +/-20 of its 1T number. Confidence low. **Clerical restatement 2026-09-11, before any game - the prediction is unchanged, only its 1T reference is now measured.** RAR-M45 resolved the 1T numbers to Houdini 3 -224, Critter 1.6a -184, Fritz 16 -147, Rybka 4 -99, Basilisk 1.9.3 -9, so the frozen band resolves to: **Houdini 3 -234 to -264, Critter 1.6a -194 to -224, Fritz 16 -157 to -187, Rybka 4 -109 to -139, Basilisk 1.10.0 -43 to -3** (the 1.9.3 band of -29 to +11 is superseded by decision (1) above, which substitutes the opponent). No bound, clock, book or confidence changes; this only makes the existing prediction scorable against the pool that now exists. **Clerical clarification, 2026-09-10, before any game: Phase A was reordered.** The Rarog arm is now the **A.7 version-bumped 2.4.0 binary**, which A.9 then publishes, so the pool describes the released engine. That binary also carries A.4's changes, which are behaviour-neutral by contract and must read `bench 13` = **7,601,220 / EBF 2.474**; if the arm's fingerprint is not that number the run is void and the discrepancy is the finding. No bound, opponent, clock, book or prediction changes. **RESULT - RUN COMPLETE 2026-09-11, Colosseum tournament `dfb84c19`.** 2,800 of 2,800 games in **2h30m** (18.7 games/min, against the 18.8 estimated). Config read back from the stored `config_json` and matching the registration exactly: gauntlet seeds 1 cycles 200, `games_per_pair` 2, `concurrency` 3, `threads` 4, `hash_mb` 512, all three adjudication fields null. All 1,400 opening pairs exactly two games. **Zero time forfeits. One engine crash - Houdini 3, exit `0xc0000005`, round 172, loss awarded** (+1 game to Rarog on a 400-game pair, about +2 Elo on that pair alone, immaterial to the +55 shift below but recorded). Rarog itself: zero crashes, zero forfeits in 2,800 games at 4T. **Overall 848-726-1226 = 43.25%; Rarog performance rating 3034 against its frozen 3003, so +31.** **Head-to-head, paired 95% intervals, with the 1T number and the shift:** Rybka 4 **-73 +/-28** (107-103-190), shift **+26**; Critter 1.6a **-109 +/-27** (90-98-212), shift **+75**; Fritz 16 **-149 +/-30** (73-92-235), shift **-2**; Houdini 3 **-169 +/-31** (70-79-251), shift **+55**; Basilisk 1.10.0 **+25 +/-27** (154-121-125), shift **+48**; Rarog 2.3.2 **+45 +/-25** (150-152-98), shift **-25**; Rybka 3 **+79 +/-28** (204-81-115), shift **-3**. A shift combines two intervals, so significance needs about +/-38: **+75, +55 and +48 are significant; +26 and -25 are not; -3 and -2 are flat.** **ALL SEVEN PREDICTIONS MISSED, and the four-target prediction missed in SIGN.** It said 4T deficits would be 10 to 40 Elo WORSE than 1T; they are **better** against three of the four targets and unchanged against the fourth. Predicted performance rating 2963-2993, measured **3034**. **V1 - 4T/1T NPS scaling, PASSES.** Rarog **5.80x**, far above the >=3.0 bar, and no opponent is below 1.5x, so no row is a handicap comparison: Basilisk 1.10.0 5.85x, Rarog 2.3.2 5.58x, Fritz 16 5.12x, Rybka 3 3.92x, Houdini 3 3.50x, Rybka 4 3.48x, Critter 1.6a 3.32x. Depth gained: Rarog +1.89 ply, Basilisk +1.77, Fritz 16 +2.14, Critter +1.50, Houdini 3 +1.32, Rybka 3 +1.32, Rybka 4 +1.23. *These ratios fold the hash rise in with the thread rise and are not thread scaling alone.* **White/black NPS symmetry - no harness asymmetry.** Five of seven sit within 1.5% (Rarog 1.005, Rarog 2.3.2 1.000, Basilisk 0.988, Critter 1.015, Houdini 3 0.968) and the deviations have mixed sign, which is what rules out a scheduler starving one side - a real harness effect would push every engine the same way. The two outliers, **Fritz 16 1.184 and Rybka 4 1.093**, are therefore engine-specific; both are engines whose reported node counts are known not to be plain node counts, so their NPS figures should not be compared with the others' and their scaling ratios carry no weight as evidence. **Calibration, appended after exposure; the prediction is not rewritten.** **The miss is in SIGN, which is the most serious kind, and the failed premise is identified.** The 2026-09-09 prediction rested on 'the current lazy SMP has never been tuned for quality and the opponents' SMP is mature'. The second half is false for this field: these are 2008-2012 engines built for two to four cores, and Rarog converts four threads better than every one of them. **The tension was registered prospectively on 2026-09-11, before any game**, from the 2026-08-01 NPS scaling, and the run confirms it was real - so the process worked even though the number did not. **That is not a claim of foresight:** the row declined to change the frozen prediction, which was correct, but the two predictions it *did* freshly register that day also missed (Rybka 3 +82 to +120, measured +79; Rarog 2.3.2 +50 to +90, measured +45). Identifying the right mechanism did not produce right numbers. **Neither speed proxy explains the pattern, and that is the finding.** Against the engines with credible node counts the story is monotone - Critter scales 3.32x and Rarog gains +75, Houdini 3 3.50x and +55, Fritz 16 5.12x and +0 - **but Basilisk 1.10.0 scales 5.85x, essentially identical to Rarog's 5.80x, and Rarog still gains +48 against it.** Same family, same scaling, same depth gain within 0.12 ply, and a 48-Elo swing that flips the sibling comparison from -23 to **+25**. Rarog converts threads into better decisions by a mechanism neither NPS nor depth captures. **That is B.0/D.2's question, not this row's answer.** **D.2's premise is contradicted and the leaf must be re-scoped before it consumes work.** It is registered as recovering strength from immature lazy SMP; at 4T against this field there is no such deficit to recover. **This does NOT establish that Rarog's SMP is good in absolute terms** - it establishes that it beats the SMP of engines designed for two to four cores, which is a low bar, and says nothing about G.1's 8/16/32 threads, where lazy SMP characteristically degrades. The cheap re-justification is a self-scaling curve (Rarog 1T/2T/4T/8T against one fixed opponent), which measures Rarog's own scaling without needing a donor to compare against. **A planning assumption inverts: the E.2 gate's binding arm is 1T, not 4T.** E.2 needs 50% against Critter, Houdini 3, Rybka 4 and Fritz 16 at both thread counts. The 4T deficits are -109, -169, -73 and -149 against 1T's -184, -224, -99 and -147, so for three of the four engines **4T is the easier arm by 26 to 75 Elo**. Fritz 16 is the exception and is identical on both. Phase B and C should be judged against the 1T column.
