- **Research question:** the rule beats C.5.2's split statically on both sides of the six-to-seven-men boundary and removes it (RAR-E45), but it scores more Syzygy wins under +100 cp than the head in KBP-KB and KBP-KBP. Does it cost wins when the engine plays them, in particular in KBPP-KB, where RAR-E26's count rule lost conversion through drift and simplification (RAR-E27)?
- **Hypothesis / proposed mechanism:** free passers restore the gradient the count rule flattened: a KBPP-KB ending with two free passers takes `s = 38` (the count rule's 26), so the strong side's moves that keep the passers free stay distinguishable to the search, and conversion holds; in KBP-KB the search finds the promotion whatever the level (RAR-E27: 253 of 253 under the count rule).
- **Competing hypotheses:** (1) *flattening again:* positions with blockaded passers drop to `s` of 14 to 22, and KBPP-KB drifts to the ply limit or trades into insufficient material as under the count rule. (2) *a free-passer oscillation:* `s` jumps by 8 when the defending king or bishop touches a path, so the search chases or avoids those squares and loses tempo. (3) *neutral:* the search converts whatever the factor, and only the drawn overclaim moves.
- **Interacting mechanisms / consumers:** the search's pruning margins and rule-50 damping, consuming the scaled score; the eval cache and TT (the factor is a pure function of the position); the texel delta scale (follows the evaluator); `tools/diag/ocb_scale_screen.py`'s offline copy of the rule (follows if the change lands); C.5.6 and C.5.7; GUIDE's closed boundary hold.

- **Disposition:** **fails the rule in KBPP-KB; C.5.2.3 closes `NO_CHANGE`.** The two-regressor rule repeats RAR-E27's result almost exactly (−2.25 against −1.99 points, by the same two routes), so the free-passer term did not restore what a lower factor costs the strong side in KBPP-KB: with the score flattened, the search trades its bishop for passers into insufficient material or drifts to the ply limit. It halves KBPP-KB's drawn overclaim, as the count rule did: the trade sits inside one family again. The branch `c5-ocb2` is deleted; the rule is inline above and the patch in the artifacts. GUIDE's boundary hold stays closed on RAR-E29's measurement. Not Elo.
- **PREDICTION CALIBRATION (appended after exposure):**
  - Original prediction (not rewritten): items 1 to 3 above.
  - Observed: (1) KBP-KB hit, KBP-KBP hit, **KBPP-KB miss** (probability 0.5 of holding). (2) hit: KBPP-KB overclaim −33.8 points. (3) the pass (probability 0.45) did not happen.
  - Which assumption failed: that free passers keep the gradient where the count rule flattened it. Two free passers do lift `s`, but the lost games are positions where the passers are not yet free, and there the factor is lower than the head's split rule gave (the head scores six-man pure endings by the old `32 + 4·pawns + 4·passers`). The mechanism is the level of the factor below the boundary, not its shape.
- **Conditional lesson:** in these endings any factor much below the head's at six men costs KBPP-KB conversion through simplification; a rule that keeps six-man levels near the head's and still removes the jump would have to rise toward the boundary, not fall.
- **Retry trigger or `closed`:** closed `NO_CHANGE`; retry on a game record showing a cost at the boundary, or with a KBPPKB recogniser that knows which positions are won (C.5.6), which would let a lower factor apply only to the drawn ones.
