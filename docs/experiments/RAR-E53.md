# RAR-E53 — KNN-KP retried: the pawn held first, the king driven second

Indexed under *5. Evaluation and data experiments* in [`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`](../EXPERIMENTS.md).

- **Date / owner:** registered 2026-10-12, before any number was computed; agent-run (zero games). Maintainer instruction 2026-10-12 to retry after RAR-E52.
- **Baseline SHA / candidate SHA / dirty-diff hash:** baseline the current head, engine `38cbb02` (**14,197,472 / EBF 2.557**), `analysis/artifacts/c523/rarog-head.exe`. Candidate: RAR-E52's code (`analysis/artifacts/c57knnkp/candidate.patch`, `bee6e432…`: the KNN-KP evaluation, its hook in `scale_endgame`, the texel delta scale and four unit tests), re-applied, with the constants below. Reference: RAR-E51's Stockfish runs (`analysis/artifacts/c57/ref/`).
- **What RAR-E52 showed:** on the development cohort (seed 7100710, 46 clean wins at 60k) the head converts 9. With the corner drive at least as strong as the pawn term, every variant lost the pawn far more often (insufficient material 4 → 20 to 23) and converted 4 to 8. The drive works (DTZ progress 0.507 → 0.65, ply-limit failures 28 → 13 to 16); the pawn was traded for king progress.
- **Hypothesis:** the plan is ordered: hold the pawn (blockade it low), then drive the king. A pawn term that dominates the drive keeps the first part and lets the drive do the second, so conversion rises above the head's.
- **Competing explanations:** (1) *still a plan:* releasing the blockade at the right moment, to avoid stalemate and give the pawn the move that allows mate, cannot be expressed by a monotone pawn penalty; a dominant penalty would trade insufficient-material losses for stalemates and ply-limit draws. (2) *the drive is not needed:* the gain, if any, comes from the pawn term alone; the smaller `CORNER` point tests that.
- **Mechanism, interactions, invariants, tests:** as RAR-E52 (the same code and the same `BASE = 5·ADVANCE + 20`, so the score stays at 20 or more and KNN-K, scored 0, never looks better); `KINGS = 10`.

  - The best variant converts 1 more than the head (10 against 9), so **the stop rule fires** again. The head's re-read reproduces RAR-E52's exactly. The engine change was reverted.
  - *Diagnosis* (`knight_value.log`, one-ply scores from the knights' side, the defending king on d4 next to the c4 knight): with the pawn on e5, the head scores KNN-KP **+400** and the same position without the a3 knight (KN-KP) **+184**; the variant scores KNN-KP **+219** and KN-KP +184. With the pawn on e3: head +132, variant +94, both KN-KP 0. **The replacement score puts the second knight at 35 cp** (the head: 216), so the search gives a knight away for king or pawn progress and the win ends in insufficient material.
- **Disposition:** refuted in this form, and the failure of RAR-E52 and RAR-E53 has one cause, now measured: a score that *replaces* the evaluation for one material configuration has to stay above every configuration one capture away (KN-KP, which the generic evaluation scores at about a knight less a pawn, and KNN-K at 0). RAR-E52's and RAR-E53's base (70 to 620 cp less the pawn's advance, with nothing for the knights) did not, at any grid point, for an advanced pawn. The pawn-dominant grid lowered the insufficient-material losses (18 to 10 as the pawn term grew) but not to the head's 4. The corner drive and the pawn term themselves were never tested apart from this defect. KNN-KP stays as RAR-E51 left it. Not Elo.
- **PREDICTION CALIBRATION (appended after exposure):**
  - Original prediction (not rewritten): items 1 to 3 above.
  - Observed: (1) best +3 or more, **miss** (+1); insufficient material in the best variant 8 or fewer, **miss** (18). (2) and (3) not read (stop rule).
  - Which assumption failed: RAR-E52's diagnosis (the pawn traded for king progress) was the wrong one, and this registration inherited it. The losses were knights, not pawns: the design's own base made the second knight nearly worthless, a defect in the mechanism that neither registration's interaction check covered (it listed the KNN-K neighbour, not KN-KP). The miss is in mechanism.
- **Conditional lesson:** a replacement score is an interaction with every neighbouring material state; check its value against each one-capture neighbour before any game or playout, which here was a four-position, one-second check.
- **Retry trigger or `closed`:** closed in this form. A retry adds the drive and the pawn term to the generic evaluation instead of replacing it (so every material neighbour keeps its normal difference), or anchors the replacement above KN-KP's score, and checks the one-capture neighbours before the development read; same cohort, stop rule and final read.
