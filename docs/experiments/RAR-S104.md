# RAR-S104 — C.10.4: one long SPSA run over 82 coordinates, 4,000 iterations × 30 games, doubled perturbation, halved gain ratio

Indexed under *3. Search and selectivity* in [`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`](../EXPERIMENTS.md).

- **Date / owner:** registered 2026-10-20, before any game. The run is the maintainer's command (about 24 hours at 5,000 games an hour); it replaces RAR-S103, stopped unread at 80 iterations on the maintainer's instruction.
- **Kind:** PLAN C.10.4, the joint search tune on the frozen classical evaluation, in one run instead of rule 7c's chain of 2,000-iteration blocks (maintainer decision 2026-10-20, recorded under rule 7c). It accepts nothing: theta is the run's rounded final centres, baked only for C.10.5's SPRT.
- **Why this shape and not rule 7c's (the maintainer asked for the analysis outside the rule's defaults).** The pilot (RAR-S101) and RAR-E25's block say the surface is near its optimum and its gradients are small, so the question is how to resolve a small gradient and land close to the optimum, not how to travel far. In SPSA each iteration moves every coordinate by `r_k · c_k · Δ_k · sign`, where `Δ_k` is the two arms' result difference over the 30 games, `c_k` the perturbation and `r_k = a_k / c_k²` the gain ratio. On this harness `Δ_k`'s noise is about 3.9 points (30 games, about half drawn), and a gradient of `g` Elo per b43 step gives `E[Δ_k] ≈ 0.086 · g · (c_k / step)` points (30 games × 0.00144 points per Elo per game × the two arms' Elo gap `2 g c_k / step`). So:
  - *Signal and noise in the travel:* the noise travel grows as `r c · 3.9 · √N` and the signal travel as `r c · 0.086 g (c / step) · N`. Doubling `N` buys √2 in signal-to-noise. Doubling `c` doubles the signal (the arms are further apart) and leaves the noise, so it buys 2 for free, as long as the response stays near linear over ±c and the arms stay inside their ranges. Halving `r` scales signal and noise alike and halves the end point's random-walk spread.
  - *The standard block* (`c` from one b43 step decaying to 0.46, `r-end` 0.0031, N 2,000): a coordinate with no gradient travels about 0.25 step; a full-step move needs `g` of about 3 Elo per step; the journal's z sees about 1.
  - *This run* (`c` from two b43 steps decaying to 0.86, `r-end` 0.0016, N 4,000): the per-iteration move at a given `Δ` is unchanged (`r` halved, `c` doubled), so the noise travel is √2 × 0.25 ≈ 0.35 step over 4,000 iterations; the signal is ×2 from `c` and ×2 from N, so a full-step move needs `g` of about 0.75 Elo per step, and z resolves about 0.35 Elo per step. Against the standard block that is 2.8 times the resolution for 2.2 times the games.
  - *Why one run rather than two chained blocks:* a chained block restarts the gain schedule, which helps when the seeds are far (RAR-S78 found +13 Elo that way) and only re-inflates the early random walk when they are near; a single run keeps averaging as its gain decays, which is what SPSA's convergence needs. The mid-run reading is not lost: `colosseum-cli spsa history <run dir>` prints the centre vector after every completed iteration without touching the run, and the maintainer can stop at any time.
  - *Games per iteration:* at a fixed budget, more games an iteration with fewer iterations leaves the accumulated signal and noise unchanged (both scale the same way), and 30 keeps the 15 slots full. Kept.
  - *The other coordinates' perturbations* are noise for each coordinate's estimate: 81 of them at ±2 steps add about 18 Elo of spread between the arms if slopes are about 0.5 Elo per step (0.8 points, 2% more noise), and about 70 Elo if they are 2 Elo per step (3 points, 27% more); the pilot says the slopes are small, so the doubled perturbation keeps most of its gain. Both arms are perturbed symmetrically, so the comparison stays fair.
  - *Bias:* a larger perturbation measures the average slope over ±range/8 rather than ±range/16, and the end point's bias grows with `c²` for a curved surface; for finding a broad optimum of smooth margins that is acceptable, and C.10.5's gate measures the result.
  - *Rails:* `CoreSingExactSpan` (seed 4, floor 1) and `QsSeeBadFloor` (seed −43, ceiling 0) keep the b43 step so their perturbations never clamp; a clamped perturbation biases the estimate, and the reader counts clamped steps as a wire.
  - *The movement unit stays the b43 step* for every reading and rule ("a full step" is range/16 as in every earlier tune), read with `pilot_reaudit.py --movement-config tools/spsa_configs/config_c10pilot.json`; the run's own steps are twice that.
