# RAR-E26 — C.5.1 opposite-bishop scale: Rarog's rule refitted against outcomes on `hce-v4-tb`, read on held-out rows and on Syzygy-labelled endings

Indexed under *5. Evaluation and data experiments* in [`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`](../EXPERIMENTS.md).

- **Date / owner:** registered 2026-10-09, after the scores were dumped, the offline rule verified and the cohorts counted, and before any loss was computed on them; agent-run (zero games, static evaluation only).
- **Baseline SHA / candidate SHA / dirty-diff hash:** branch `c4` at `d4b4ffa`; the evaluation is `master`'s `cc13320` (fingerprint **13,187,295 / EBF 2.546**), unchanged on the branch. No candidate binary: candidates are rules applied offline to the dumped scores.
- **Binary / compiler / PGO identity:** `tools/texel-tuner` built at `d4b4ffa` (`rarog-texel.exe`, the `texel` feature in its own workspace, `rustc 1.98.1`); `rarog-texel --dump-scores <split> <out> --scale` over `tools/texel/data/hce-v4-tb/train.csv` (3,500,000 rows) and `validation.csv` (194,444 rows), the played evaluation with the lazy gate. The validation scores are byte-identical to a dump without `--scale`. The frozen test file is not read.
- **Research question:** Rarog scales the whole score by `s/48` whenever each side has one bishop and the two are on opposite colours, whatever else is on the board, with `s = min(32 + 4·pawns + 4·passers, 48)` over both sides. So a pure bishop ending with four or more pawns is not scaled at all. C.0.4 found Rarog's score scaled by the donor's factor predicting outcomes better in pure opposite-bishop endings at seven men or more (+2.13 ± 0.58% on 3,427 rows, as a second regressor, on the 2.4.0 evaluation; `analysis/c04_king_unit_2026-10-06.md` (c)), and nothing with other pieces (−0.16 ± 0.07). Is Rarog's own rule miscalibrated on the current evaluation, and does a refit of its constants, with the rest of the evaluation fixed, predict held-out outcomes better (C.5.1's first step, PLAN)?
- **Hypothesis / proposed mechanism:** pure opposite-bishop endings with several pawns are far more drawish than the evaluation's pawn balance says, and the rule's `4·pawns` term, which switches the scale off at four pawns, has the sign of the mechanism backwards for this cohort. A refit inside the rule's form (an intercept and per-pawn and per-passer steps, capped at 48) on the pure cohort scales typical pure endings down and gains held-out loss there.
- **Competing hypotheses:** (1) *absorbed:* the C.2 and C.3 refits, fitted with the rule as a fixed multiplier, already moved the material and pawn terms to absorb most of the miscalibration, so the pure cohort gains under 1%. (2) *form:* the information is in which side owns the passers (the donor counts the strong side's), not in the totals, so the strong-side form beats the total form clearly. (3) *pieces:* the miscalibration is as large with other pieces on the board, where the rule also fires. (4) *label artefact:* self-play games at 8,000 nodes draw or lose these endings for search reasons a static scale cannot fix; the train fit then fails to transfer to held-out rows.
- **Interacting mechanisms / consumers:** the evaluation terms fitted with the rule fixed (a changed rule ideally triggers C.5's cluster refit, which this screen does not run); the search's rule-50 damping, applied after the scale; the lazy gate, decided on the unscaled score and unchanged by any candidate; the eval cache and TT, which store the scaled score (position-pure, unaffected). The specialised scale functions pre-empt the rule only in pawnless minor-against-minor endings, which are excluded. Consumers: C.5.2's first candidate, C.5.6 (KBPsK, KBPPKB are opposite-bishop-capable families) and C.5.8's gate.
- **PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION (freeze before exposure):** gains in percent of the head rule's held-out mean squared error inside each cohort, paired by row, ± one standard error; K fixed at 1.54638 (C.3's fit). Cohorts: *pure* (the two bishops and pawns only; 72,904 train, 4,097 held-out rows) and *pieces* (opposite bishops with any other non-pawn piece; 187,229 and 10,406). Forms fitted on the training rows by exhaustive search over integers `a ∈ 0..48`, `b ∈ 0..12`, `c ∈ 0..12`, `s = clamp(a + b·pawns + c·q, 0, 48)`: F1 with `q` the passers of both sides (the head's form), F2 with `q` the passers of the side the unscaled score favours.
  1. Pure, F1: **+3.0%**, 80% band [+0.8, +8.0]; probability of at least +1.0% at three standard errors 0.65.
  2. Pure, the fitted F1 rule scales a pure ending with five pawns and no passer to `s ≤ 40` (probability 0.75): the head gives it 48.
  3. Pure, F2 against F1: within two standard errors of the paired difference (probability 0.6).
  4. Pieces, F1: under +0.3% (probability 0.7); switching the rule off there (`s = 48`) moves the pieces cohort's loss by under ±0.3% (probability 0.7).
  5. The pure cohort's gain is under 0.10% of the whole held-out file's squared error (probability 0.9): this is a small population.
  6. If a candidate is selected, the static read on Syzygy-labelled opposite-bishop positions (below) lowers the share of draws scored above +100 cp by at least 10 points in KBP-KB (probability 0.6) and raises the share of wins scored under +100 cp by at most 10 points in each family (probability 0.6).
  - Expected Elo sign/range, if defensible: none. Static loss ranks candidates and accepts nothing.
  - Probability positive/useful and confidence basis: 0.65 (prediction 1). Basis: C.0.4's +2.13% as a second regressor on the older evaluation; the head rule leaves 70% of pure rows unscaled; against it, two refits since then.
  - Most likely failure mode: competing (1), absorption, or a gain concentrated in the few rows with one to three pawns, where the head already scales.
- **Falsification criteria:** the screen is void if `ocb_scale_screen.py verify` fails on either dump (it passed before registration: 3,694,444 rows, no opposite-bishop mismatch, the 13,311 other rows with another factor all of five men or fewer). H1 is refuted if the pure F1 gain is under +1.0% or under three standard errors; its sign clause is refuted if the fitted pure rule's per-pawn step `b` is 4 or more.
- **Cheapest prior falsifier:** the census above (done): the pure cohort is 2.1% of the held-out rows and 70% of it carries four pawns or more, so the head rule is inactive on most of it.
- **Registered gate and stop rule:** no gate. One `analyse` run on the two dumps; at most one `drawn` read, of the selected candidate only; no form, cohort or threshold added after the numbers are seen. **Use of the readings, frozen:**
  (i) The pure-cohort candidate is the F1 rule if it gains at least +1.0% at three standard errors, or F2 instead if F2 also passes and beats F1 by at least two standard errors of their paired difference.
  (ii) The pieces cohort keeps the head's constants unless its own F1 refit gains at least +0.5% at three standard errors; a pieces change is then part of the same candidate.
  (iii) With a candidate, the static `drawn` read runs on `KBP-KB`, `KBPP-KB` and `KBP-KBP` (2,000 generated positions each at `endgame_truth.py`'s seed `0x5E9D18`, opposite-bishop positions kept, labelled by `syzygy3456`), head rule against candidate at +100 cp. It is a veto: if the candidate raises the share of wins scored under +100 cp by more than ten points in a family with at least 25 wins, it goes to the maintainer before C.5.2 builds it.
  (iv) Without a candidate, C.5.2's opposite-bishop part closes `NO_CHANGE` on this record, and C.5.2 stays open only for what C.5.1's classification supports.
  (v) A selected candidate is C.5.2's implementation input: the rule's constants and its split, not a strength claim; its game evidence is C.5's (C.5.8).

  - **Pure, F2** `(0, 2, 12)`: +21.13 ± 1.29%; over F1 +0.12 ± 0.39%.
  - **Pure, no scaling:** −1.68 ± 0.12% (the head's rule helps slightly where it fires).
  - **Pieces:** F1 `(46, 0, 2)` +0.075 ± 0.025%; F2 `(32, 12, 0)` +0.009 ± 0.007%; no scaling 0.000 ± 0.011%.
  - Grid edges: `a = 0` is the grid's floor in both pure forms and F2's `c = 12` is its ceiling; the registered grid is not widened.

- **Disposition:** by the frozen use: (i) the pure-cohort candidate is **F1, `s = clamp(2·pawns + 10·passers, 0, 48)`** (+21.0% at 15.6 standard errors; F2 does not beat it by two). (ii) The pieces cohort keeps the head's `32 + 4·pawns + 4·passers` (+0.075% is under the +0.5% bar). (iii) **The veto fired:** the candidate raises the wins scored under +100 cp by 24.9 points in KBP-KB (253 wins) and 16.7 in KBP-KBP (203). So the candidate goes to the maintainer before C.5.2 builds it. H1 holds: the gain is far above +1.0%, and the fitted per-pawn step is 2, under 4. Competing (1) (absorbed) is refuted; (2) (strong-side form) is not supported; (3) (pieces) is refuted. (4) (label artefact) is **not tested** by this read, because the held-out rows come from the same generator. At six men or fewer the labels are Syzygy truth, and there the one-pawn rows are 98.8% drawn, as theory says. The static read is the independent evidence, and it shows the trade the outcome loss accepts: a count-only factor cannot separate won from drawn KBP-KB, and the generated cohort (26% wins in KBP-KB) weighs the wins far more than games do. Not Elo: static loss ranks candidates and accepts nothing.
- **PREDICTION CALIBRATION (appended after exposure):**
  - Original prediction (not rewritten): items 1 to 6 above.
  - Observed: (1) **miss in magnitude**, sign right: +21.0 against +3.0 and a band topping at +8.0. The ≥1.0%-at-three-SE event (0.65) happened. (2) hit: `s = 10` at five pawns and no passer. (3) hit: +0.12 ± 0.39. (4) hit on both clauses. (5) **miss:** +0.24% of the whole file against under 0.10% (0.9). (6) first clause hit (−57.5 points); second **miss** in two families.
  - Which assumption failed: I scaled the prior from C.0.4's +2.13%, which was a *second regressor* fitted linearly beside Rarog's score. A linear blend can recover only a fraction of a missing multiplicative factor. I also assumed the two refits could absorb some of it, but additive material and pawn terms cannot stand in for a factor that applies to one material class. The instrument behind the prior was too weak for this question, and the miss is in magnitude and confidence (over-confident on the narrow band), not mechanism. For (5) and (6) I underrated how large the cohort's squared error is per row once a whole class is mis-scaled, and I did not foresee that a count-only factor must trade wins for draws in a family that is mostly drawn.
- **Conditional lesson:** a residual read as an extra regressor understates what replacing a mis-specified rule recovers. Size a substitution's prior from the rule's own inactivity (here, 70% of the cohort unscaled), not from the regressor's gain.
