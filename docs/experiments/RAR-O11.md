# RAR-O11 — Where Basilisk's search programme gained more: the start points, per-node quality against speed, and evaluation cost

Indexed under *3. Search and selectivity › Search-oracle observations* in [`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`](../EXPERIMENTS.md).

- **Date / owner:** registered 2026-11-14 (PLAN D.7.2.2). Preparation and the analysis are the agent's; the night run is the maintainer's command (about an hour of compute).
- **Kind:** observation, zero games. It decides nothing by itself; it is an input to the maintainer's decision on D.7's donor and on D.7.3 to D.7.9.
- **Research question:** Rarog and Basilisk followed the same process (a donor-shaped search rebuilt cluster by cluster, fitted and gated; Basilisk's PLAN B cites Rarog's clusters as its reason) from level starts (Rarog 2.4.0, Basilisk 1.10.2), and Basilisk's search programme gained more: +318.1 ± 24.5 against +252.6 ± 20.4 (RAR-M84), and about +44 since in self-play, still open. RAR-O10 then found Basilisk's evaluation less accurate than Rarog's while its moves at equal time are about level, with a ply more depth at about the same nodes. Which of three explanations carries the difference: (1) Basilisk started with a weaker search and had more room; (2) the donor's shape (Stockfish 19 against Reckless); (3) the fit and the build. And separately: is any of Basilisk's strength raw speed, and what does an evaluation cost in each engine and in Stockfish's classical evaluation?
- **What this read can and cannot separate:** it measures the start points and the ends of both search programmes on one instrument, at equal time and at equal nodes, which addresses (1) and the speed question. It does not separate (2) from (3); that is D.7.2.3's ledger comparison and, if a use is named, a Stockfish 19 hybrid read.
- **Instrument:**
  1. *Positions and reference.* RAR-O10's 10,000 positions and its Stockfish 19 labels (1,000,000 nodes, Hash 64, one thread, `ucinewgame`, expected score (W + D/2) / 1000); the positions after moves that RAR-O10 did not label are labelled the same way in this run. Loss is the reference's expected score at the root minus its expected score after the move, zero for the reference's own move (RAR-O10's definition).
  2. *(a) Moves.* Five engines, each its own search and evaluation: Rarog 2.4.0 and 2.5.0 (release binaries, the start and the end of Rarog's search programme, one evaluation), Rarog's head (RAR-O09's arm, `id name` dev-20261114-1f29e048), Basilisk 1.10.2 (release) and Basilisk's head (RAR-O10's copy, dev-20261114-8bfbbfff, mid-programme). Each at `go movetime 100` and at `go nodes 150000` on every position, Hash 64, one thread, `ucinewgame` per position, 14 processes at once (RAR-O10's procedure). Recorded: the move, depth, nodes, reported time and wall time.
  3. *(b) Evaluation cost.* Four timers, each built from its revision's own source and calling the evaluation its search calls: Rarog 2.4.0 and head (`Evaluator::evaluate`, Cargo release, LTO, `target-cpu=native`, no PGO), Basilisk 1.10.2 and head (`Evaluator::evaluate`, clang 23.1.5 `-O2 -march=native`, no PGO). Each pass evaluates the 10,000 positions once with a fresh evaluator, so no answer comes from an earlier pass's cache; 21 passes a run, the first not read, three rounds in rotated order, one process at a time. And one search with four evaluations: the oracle hybrid (Stockfish's classical search, one executable in every arm) with Stockfish's classical evaluation and with Rarog's 2.4.0, C11 and head evaluations through its adapter DLL, at `go nodes 150000` on the first 2,800 positions not in check, each worker searching each position with every arm in rotated order.
  4. *(c) Static accuracy at the start.* Basilisk 1.10.2's evaluation scored beside Rarog 2.4.0's and both heads' by RAR-O10's quiet split, fit and skill.
- **Preparation checks (2026-11-14):** the Rarog timers reproduce RAR-O10's head evaluations and the 2.4.0 DLL's on all 10,000 positions; the Basilisk head timer reproduces RAR-O10's Basilisk values on all 10,000. Basilisk 1.10.2's timer is built from its tagged source and differs from its head on 9,698 of 10,000 positions, so its evaluation changed after 1.10.2; no second 1.10.2 path was compared. A wiring smoke on 28 positions (`smoke/`, 3 timer passes) ran every step to the end.
- **Exposure before this registration, recorded:** the smoke computed (c) in full, since (c) needs no new data: Basilisk 1.10.2 skill 0.1524, Rarog 2.4.0 0.1700, difference +0.0176 [+0.0070, +0.0300]; Basilisk's head 0.1647, +0.0123 [+0.0071, +0.0180] over 1.10.2. So (c) carries no prediction. The smoke's (b) values were seen at smoke size (Rarog head about 420 ns a call against 2.4.0's 610; in the hybrid, Stockfish's evaluation about 620 ns a node against 1,170 for Rarog 2.4.0's), so (b) carries no prediction either. The smoke's 28-position (a) differences were seen and are uninformative at that size.
- **Interacting mechanisms / confounds:** loss against a reference is not Elo (RAR-X03). The engines may count nodes differently (Rarog counts a null move's child, RAR-O09), so "equal nodes" is approximate across engines and exact within one. 100 ms a move on one thread is not a game clock. The timers are not PGO builds, while the engines are. A full uncached evaluation is dearer than a search's average evaluation, where the pawn cache hits; the timer ranks cost, it does not give a node's share. The hybrid's time a node includes the adapter's board conversion and each evaluation's own tree.
- **PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION (frozen 2026-11-14, before the night run; (a) only):**
  - Start points, equal time: Basilisk 1.10.2's mean loss within ±10% of Rarog 2.4.0's: 0.45; Basilisk's higher by more than 10%: 0.30; Rarog's higher by more than 10%: 0.25.
  - Start points, equal nodes: Basilisk 1.10.2 loses more than Rarog 2.4.0, interval excluding zero: 0.50.
  - Search programmes' ends, equal nodes: Basilisk's head loses less than Rarog 2.5.0, interval excluding zero: 0.55.
  - Heads: Basilisk's ratio to Rarog at equal time within 5% of its ratio at equal nodes (speed is not what separates them): 0.60.
  - Each search programme lowers its engine's loss at both limits, intervals excluding zero: Rarog (2.5.0 against 2.4.0) 0.85, Basilisk (head against 1.10.2) 0.85. Rarog's evaluation programme (head against 2.5.0) lowers loss at equal nodes, interval excluding zero: 0.80.
  - Most likely failure: the start-point differences fall inside their intervals, which then says only that neither engine started far ahead in per-node quality.
- **Use of the reading, frozen:**
  - *Start points.* At equal nodes Basilisk 1.10.2 losing more than 10% more than Rarog 2.4.0, interval excluding zero, while within 10% at equal time: Basilisk's level start rested on speed, its search had more room per node, and explanation (1) gains weight. Level or better at equal nodes: (1) loses weight; the larger gain lies in the donor or the fit, and D.7.2.3, then the Stockfish 19 hybrid read, are what separates those.
  - *Speed.* For each pair, the equal-time ratio over the equal-node ratio above 1.05 or below 0.95: speed moves that comparison by more than 5%, in that direction; inside: speed is not what separates them.
  - *Evaluation cost.* The timers rank the four evaluations' cost and give the programmes' cost change; the hybrid gives Stockfish's classical evaluation against Rarog's in one search. Both feed D.12.0's ranking and the balance question (about 2 Elo per 1% NPS at `3+0.03`); neither accepts or rejects an evaluation.
  - None opens or closes a leaf by itself; all go to the maintainer's decision on D.7's donor and on D.7.3 to D.7.9.
- **Stop rules:** a step that fails stops the run; a host not idle within eight hours stops it before the step it waits for; no re-run on the same binaries and positions.
