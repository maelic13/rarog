- **The tune's shape, considered (the maintainer asked whether a different iteration count, games per iteration or step size would find the optimum better when the surface is mostly noise):**
  - *Games per iteration:* at a fixed budget, 60 games × 1,000 iterations halves the per-iteration gradient noise by √2 and halves the iterations; the accumulated signal and the accumulated noise both come out the same as 30 × 2,000, so nothing is gained, and 30 keeps the 15 slots full. Kept at 30.
  - *Iterations:* the signal travel grows with N and the noise travel with √N, so a longer block is the one lever that raises the signal-to-noise of the movement read; rule 7c fixes a block at 2,000 (about 11 hours) and chains blocks by `-SeedFrom`, so the lever is the number of blocks, decided by hand after each one. Kept at 2,000.
  - *Perturbation (the steps):* the gradient estimate's noise falls with the perturbation size and its bias grows with it; the converter already starts each coordinate at one b43 step (range/16) and decays to 0.46 of it. Larger perturbations would raise the signal-to-noise of each estimate but move the arms further from the head and change what "a full step" means against every earlier block. Kept.
  - *Gain ratio (`r-end`):* a smaller ratio shrinks every move, noise and signal alike; it lowers the end point's variance and slows the approach, and under a weak gradient it turns a full-step move into a half-step one, which the movement rule then misses. With the seeds believed near the optimum and the journal statistic (z) independent of the gain, the validated 0.0031 is kept; a tune that reads gradient but little travel would be the case for a lower ratio in a *later* registration, never mid-tune.
  - *What the noise looks like at this shape:* over 2,000 iterations a coordinate with no gradient travels about 0.25 of a step (one standard deviation; the sum of the schedule's steps with fair random signs), consistent with RAR-S83's two blocks showing no full-step mover; a full-step move therefore needs a gradient of roughly one to two Elo per step across the two arms, and the journal's z sees about half that.
