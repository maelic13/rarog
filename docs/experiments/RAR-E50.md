# RAR-E50 — The floors read's 100-position sample: are other families unrepresentative, and how much does a per-move standard error overstate precision?

Indexed under *5. Evaluation and data experiments* in [`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`](../EXPERIMENTS.md).

- **Date / owner:** registered 2026-10-12, before any number was computed; agent-run (zero games), on the maintainer's instruction of 2026-10-12 after RAR-E49.
- **Baseline SHA / candidate SHA / dirty-diff hash:** the current head, engine `38cbb02` (**14,197,472 / EBF 2.557**), as `analysis/artifacts/c523/rarog-head.exe` (SHA-256 `b34990d3…`). Floors: `tools/diag/endgame_floors.json` at `9bb7e91`. Comparison: RAR-E48's 100-position read of the same binary, `analysis/artifacts/c56/truth-head.json`.
- **Research question:** RAR-E49 found that the floors read's 100 KRP-KB positions are slow for the current engine (DTZ progress 0.287, against 0.334 on positions 101 to 400, 3.5 SE apart under the per-move standard error), so the floors read reported a defect the engine does not have. Two questions decide what C.5.8 does about it: (1) is any other family's 100-position sample unrepresentative of its 400-position cohort; (2) how much does `endgame_floors.py`'s per-move binomial standard error understate the per-game one (the design effect), family by family?
- **Hypothesis / proposed mechanism:** moves within one game share the position's difficulty and the engine's plan, so they are positively correlated and the per-move standard error is too small; KRP-KB's 3.5 SE split is mostly that correlation, and with a per-game standard error no family's first 100 positions differ from the rest beyond chance.
- **Competing hypotheses:** (1) *some samples really are unrepresentative:* random 100-position draws from families with heterogeneous positions (pawn ranks, piece placements) can differ from the family mean beyond per-game noise, and those families need a larger floors read. (2) *the correlation is small:* the design effect is near 1, so the per-move standard error is adequate and KRP-KB's split is a genuinely unrepresentative sample.
- **Interacting mechanisms / consumers:** `endgame_floors.py` (every endgame screen's ratchet), its `REPORT_SIGMA` 2 and failure threshold, the floors' cohort size of 100; RAR-E25's waived KBP-K breach and RAR-E48/RAR-E49's re-baselines, whose reports this re-reads; C.5.8.
- **PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION (freeze before exposure):**
  1. Every family's first 100 positions of the 400 hash to the floors' digest for that family (probability 0.95).
  2. The first 100 positions of the 400-position run reproduce RAR-E48's 100-position aggregates exactly in every family (same binary, nodes, hash, seed; one thread a game) (0.8).
  3. The design effect of DTZ progress (per-game variance over per-move variance, over all 400 positions) has a median between 1.5 and 4 across the eligible families (0.6); for win preservation the median exceeds 1.5 (0.5).
  4. Under the per-game standard error, KRP-KB's first-100 against rest split in DTZ progress is under 3 SE (0.55), and at most one family in all is flagged unrepresentative (0.7).
- **Falsification criteria:** void if the head arm does not bench 14,197,472, or if any family's 400-position cohort digest's first-100 prefix differs from the floors' digest (then the subsets are not the floors' positions).
- **Cheapest prior falsifier:** the prefix check over the 19 families (seconds).
- **Registered order (frozen):** into `analysis/artifacts/floors400/`:
  1. `check_prefix.py`: for each of the 19 default families, the first 100 of the generated 400 positions against the floors' digest.
  2. The head's bench (fingerprint), then `endgame_truth.py --positions 400 --per-position` over the default 19 families at RAR-E25's settings (60,000 nodes, 100 plies, seed 6200600, hash 16, 14 workers), on an idle host, into `truth400-all.json`.
  3. `analyse.py` on it: per family and metric (conversion over clean wins; win preservation over graded moves; DTZ progress over checked moves), the rate on positions 1 to 100 and on 101 to 400, the per-move and per-game z of their difference, the design effect over all 400, and the reproduction check against `c56/truth-head.json`.
  - Nothing is added after the numbers are seen.
- **Estimators (frozen):** a rate is a ratio of sums over games, r = Σyᵢ / Σnᵢ. Its per-game variance is the linearised cluster variance, G/(G−1) · Σ(yᵢ − r·nᵢ)² / (Σnᵢ)², over the G games with nᵢ > 0; the per-move variance is r(1−r)/Σnᵢ. The design effect is their ratio over all 400 positions. Two disjoint subsets are compared by (r₂ − r₁) / √(v₁ + v₂). Conversion is one outcome per game, so its two variances coincide and it gets no design effect. A family-metric is eligible when each subset has at least 30 games with nᵢ > 0 (for conversion: 30 clean wins).
- **Rule (frozen):** a family-metric is **unrepresentative** when its per-game |z| between positions 1 to 100 and 101 to 400 is 3 or more (about 50 family-metrics are compared, so 3 rather than 2 keeps the expected false flags near 0.1). The read **supports option 2** (a per-game standard error in `endgame_floors.py`) when the median DTZ-progress design effect over eligible families is 1.5 or more, that is, when the per-move standard error understates the per-game one by a fifth or more.
- **Use, frozen:** the read recommends, it does not change anything: an unrepresentative family is recommended for a 400-position floors read; a supported option 2 is recommended as a tooling change in its own commit with tests. Both go to the maintainer at C.5.8 (which owns the floors) unless the maintainer asks for them earlier. If neither holds, the KRP-KB report is recorded as known sample noise and the floors read stays as it is.
